\section[APPENDIX A.\ SAMPLE CALCULATIONS]{Sample Calculations}
\label{app:calc}

This appendix works through each equation and calibration step of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} on recorded values from pre-proposal runs on a Google Colab Tesla T4 GPU, using speakers from the development split only. Its purpose is to show concretely how the candidate teacher paths, the two decision signals, the routing rule, the quality-failure check, and the Learn-Then-Test calibration procedure operate before confirmatory data collection begins. Sections~\ref{app:calc-grid} to~\ref{app:calc-fail} follow development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} (target sentence ``Some of the grey ash was falling off the circular edge.'', $11$ normalized words, $339$ prompt mel frames, and $572$ total frames). Section~\ref{app:calc-ltt} then steps through the three-path binomial test of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} and the minimum calibration sample size of \Cref{eq:zerofail}.

\subsection{Candidate Teacher Paths and Forward-Pass Cost}
\label{app:calc-grid}

On the 8-interval sway grid ($N=8$ in \Cref{eq:grid}), grid point $i$ sits at $t_i = 1-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\cdot\tfrac{i}{8})$. For the four-step candidate path ($k=4$ in \Cref{eq:paths}),
\[
t_4 = 1-\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{8}\Big) = 1-\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) = 1-0.7071 = 0.2929,
\]
which equals $t_8^{(16)}$ of the released 16-interval grid and coincides with student schedule point $\tau_2=0.2929$ ($j=k/2=2$). Similarly, $k=2$ gives $t_2=1-\cos(\pi/8)=0.0761=\tau_1$ ($j=1$) and $k=6$ gives $t_6=1-\cos(3\pi/8)=0.6173=\tau_3$ ($j=3$). By Proposition~\ref{prop:cost} (\Cref{eq:cost}), path $k$ makes $e=k+1$ guided teacher evaluations ($2k+2$ passes), one student draft pass ($u=1$), and $n_s=4-k/2$ student finishing passes:
\[
C(4) = 2(4+1) + 1 + (4-2) = 10 + 1 + 2 = 13\ \text{passes},
\;
\Delta C = 1-\frac{13}{21} = 38.1\%.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:app-grid} lists all 8-interval grid points up to $t_6$ alongside the released 16-interval baseline.

\subsection{Prompt SNR Estimate}

To illustrate \Cref{eq:snr} directly from frame energies, on development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901} ($459$ prompt frames of $25$~ms at $10$~ms hop; quietest $10\%=46$ frames, loudest $50\%=230$ frames), the clean prompt has $\bar E_{\text{bottom}} = 1.747\times 10^{-5}$ and $\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.043\times 10^{-2}$, giving $\hat S = 10\log_{10}(0.02043/1.747\times 10^{-5}) = 30.68$~dB ($\ge\theta=20$~dB, gate open), whereas adding $15$~dB white noise raises $\bar E_{\text{bottom}}$ to $3.563\times 10^{-4}$ ($\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.076\times 10^{-2}$) and drops $\hat S$ to $17.65$~dB ($<20$~dB, gate closed). On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358}, the same estimator gives $\hat S=29.12$~dB on the clean prompt, $\hat S=17.69$~dB under heavy white noise ($15$~dB, caught by the gate), and $\hat S=25.50$~dB under moderate white noise ($25$~dB) or $25.45$~dB under moderate high-pass hiss ($25$~dB)---both above $\theta=20$~dB, leaving moderate noise to the invariant residual.

\subsection{Endpoint Estimate, Quantile Projection, Residual, and Routing}

At the commit point $t_k$, the teacher forms $\hat x_1 = x_{t_k} + (1-t_k)\,v(x_{t_k},t_k)$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}) at no pass beyond the final guided velocity evaluation; for instance, a mel entry with state $x_{t_4}=-0.6905$ and guided velocity $v=-0.5198$ at $t_4=0.2929$ gives $\hat x_1 = -0.6905 + (1-0.2929)(-0.5198) = -1.0580$. Independently from noise, one student evaluation at $\tau=0$ produces the draft $s_0 = \varepsilon_0 + g(\varepsilon_0,0)$ (\Cref{eq:draft}).

To see how the per-band quantile projection (\Cref{eq:quantile}) and invariant residual (\Cref{eq:residual,eq:w2}) work numerically, consider four illustrative generated frames in one mel band where the clean draft has values $s_{0,b}=(-3.8,-1.2,-4.0,-2.1)$ (sorted values $s_{0,b,(1..4)}=(-4.0,-3.8,-2.1,-1.2)$) and a noise-contaminated teacher band has $\hat x_{1,b}=(-1.9,-0.8,-1.1,-2.2)$ (where quiet pause frames were lifted by copied hiss):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Teacher time ranks.} Sorting $\hat x_{1,b}$ from quietest to loudest orders frames as $(4,1,3,2)$, so the frame ranks are $\operatorname{rank}_b=(2,4,3,1)$.
\item \emph{Quantile projection (\Cref{eq:quantile}).} Placing $s_{0,b,(\operatorname{rank}_b(t))}$ at each frame $t$ yields $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)_b = (-3.8,-1.2,-2.1,-4.0)$: the teacher's loudest frame ($t=2$, rank $4$) keeps the loudest clean value ($-1.2$), while the quietest frame ($t=4$, rank $1$) is restored from $-2.2$ to $-4.0$.
\item \emph{Residual and Wasserstein identity (\Cref{eq:residual,eq:w2}).} Framewise squared differences sum to $(-1.9+3.8)^2+(-0.8+1.2)^2+(-1.1+2.1)^2+(-2.2+4.0)^2 = 3.61+0.16+1.00+3.24 = 8.01$, which equals the sorted order-statistic sum $(-2.2+4.0)^2+(-1.9+3.8)^2+(-1.1+2.1)^2+(-0.8+1.2)^2 = 8.01 = 4\,W_2^2(\hat\mu_b,\hat\nu_b)$. With $\|\hat x_{1,b}\|^2 = 10.30$, this single band has relative residual $\sqrt{8.01}/\sqrt{10.30}=0.882$.
\end{enumerate}

Over all $n=233$ generated frames and $B=100$ mel bands of \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} at $k=4$ on the Tesla T4, the recorded invariant residual is $r=0.2532$ on the clean prompt, $r=0.6844$ under high-pass $25$~dB hiss, and $r=0.7267$ under white $25$~dB noise. With an illustrative threshold $\lambda=0.35$ and $\theta=20$~dB in \Cref{eq:policy}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Clean prompt} ($\hat S=29.12\ge 20$~dB, $r=0.2532\le 0.35$): neither condition triggers, so $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=\hat x_1$ (teacher's estimate kept).
\item \emph{High-pass, 25~dB} ($\hat S=25.45\ge 20$~dB, $r=0.6844>0.35$): the SNR gate stays open, but $r>\lambda$ triggers $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ (corrected payload).
\item \emph{White, 15~dB} ($\hat S=17.69<20$~dB, $r=0.6480>0.35$): both checks trigger $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quality-Failure Rule and Excess-Failure Check}
\label{app:calc-fail}

Across the $30$ clean development prompts of the feasibility pilot (Appendix~\ref{app:snr-r}), the 5th percentiles (\Cref{eq:floors}) of the reproduced 21-pass released system are $s_{\min}=0.8482$ and $u_{\min}=3.4031$, alongside the WER ceiling $c=12.5\%$ (\Cref{eq:failure}). Applying Definition~\ref{def:failure} and the excess-failure indicator $e(x,k,\lambda)=F(x,y_{k,\lambda}(x))\,(1-F(x,y_{\text{ref}}(x)))$ (\Cref{eq:excess}) at $k=4$, $\lambda=0.35$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Clean case ($e=0$, both pass).} On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} (clean), the released system scores $\mathrm{WER}=9.09\%\le 12.5\%$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.971\ge 0.8482$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.697\ge 3.4031$ ($F_{\text{ref}}=0$), and the selected teacher payload $\hat x_1$ scores $(9.09\%,0.956,3.847)$ ($F=0$), giving $e=0\times(1-0)=0$.
\item \emph{Excess failure prevented by routing ($e=1\to 0$).} On development speaker \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_19833804-common_voice_en_19833805} under pink $25$~dB noise ($\hat S=26.94$~dB, $r=0.4535$), the 21-pass released system passes all three bars ($\mathrm{WER}=0\%$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.931$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.749$, so $F_{\text{ref}}=0$). Without projection, $\hat x_1$ drops to $\mathrm{UTMOS}=2.785<3.4031$ ($F=1$, which would count as an excess failure $e=1\times(1-0)=1$); because $r=0.4535>0.35$, the router switches to $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$, restoring $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.914$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.935$, $\mathrm{WER}=0\%$ ($F=0$, so $e=0$).
\item \emph{Damaged prompt where the released system itself fails ($F_{\text{ref}}=1\implies e=0$).} On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} under white $15$~dB noise, the 21-pass released system copies background noise ($\mathrm{SIM}=0.670<0.8482$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=1.253<3.4031$, so $F_{\text{ref}}=1$), illustrating why Definition~\ref{def:failure} does not assume a failure-free baseline; routing to $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ cuts WER from $90.9\%$ (unprojected $\hat x_1$) to $9.09\%$ and yields $e=F\times(1-1)=0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three-Path Calibration p-Values and Minimum Sample Size}
\label{app:calc-ltt}

In Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt}, $\delta=0.10$ is split equally across the $|\mathcal{K}|=3$ candidate paths, so each path tests its ordered threshold grid $\Lambda=\{0.00,0.05,\dots,1.00\}$ at level $\delta/3 = 0.0333$ using the exact binomial p-value of \Cref{eq:pvalue}. At the study's calibration size $n=200$ and $\alpha=0.05$,
\[
\P\big(\mathrm{Bin}(200,0.05)\le 4\big) = \sum_{j=0}^{4}\binom{200}{j}0.05^j\,0.95^{200-j} = 0.0287 \le 0.0333,
\]
whereas $5$ excess failures give $\P(\mathrm{Bin}(200,0.05)\le 5) = 0.0623 > 0.0333$ (matching Table~\ref{tab:samplesize}). Table~\ref{tab:app-ltt} illustrates how Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} steps through one path's ordered grid at $n=200$ and then selects $\hat k = \min\{k\in\mathcal K : \hat\lambda^{(k)}\ne\textsc{none}\}$.

\emph{Smallest calibration set with zero excess failures.} With $n\hat R_{k,g}=0$, \Cref{eq:pvalue} simplifies to $p_{k,g}=(1-\alpha)^n$. Under \Cref{eq:zerofail} with $|\mathcal{K}|=3$, $\alpha=0.05$, and $\delta=0.10$, accepting a zero-failure candidate requires
\[
0.95^n \le \frac{0.10}{3} = 0.0333
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n \ge \frac{\ln(0.10/3)}{\ln(0.95)} = \frac{-3.4012}{-0.05129} = 66.31.
\]
Indeed $0.95^{66}=0.0339>0.0333$ fails whereas $0.95^{67}=0.0322\le 0.0333$ passes, giving the $67$-speaker minimum of Section~\ref{sec:calsize} (and explaining why a $30$-speaker pilot has $0.95^{30}=0.2146$ and cannot certify $\alpha=0.05$ on its own, whereas testing a single fixed path $|\mathcal{K}|=1$ requires $\lceil\ln(0.10)/\ln(0.95)\rceil=45$ speakers).

\section[APPENDIX B.\ SAMPLE SIMULATION]{Sample Simulation}
\label{app:sim}

Before synthesizing the full $200$-speaker calibration set on GPU, a Monte Carlo simulation was conducted to verify that the three-path Learn-Then-Test procedure of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} satisfies Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}'s finite-sample guarantee $\P(R(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)\le\alpha)\ge 1-\delta$, and to compare it against a naive uncalibrated rule that simply picks the cheapest path and largest threshold whose empirical excess-failure rate is at most $\alpha$. No speech is synthesized; all numbers are reproduced deterministically by \nolinkurl{code/analysis/ltt_sim.py}.

\subsection{Setup}

Each simulated utterance draws one of the eight prompt conditions of Table~\ref{tab:conditions} with equal probability (clean; heavy low-SNR noise around $17.5$~dB; three moderate $25$~dB noises around $26.2$~dB that pass the $\theta=20$~dB SNR gate; and two channel conditions around $28.5$~dB), together with a nonzero baseline failure indicator $F_{\text{ref}}\in\{0,1\}$ matching Definition~\ref{def:failure} ($5\%$ failure on clean prompts, $55\%$--$70\%$ on degraded prompts). Across the three candidate teacher paths $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$ ($10$, $13$, and $16$ passes; fallback $21$ passes), residuals $r^{(k)}$ concentrate near $0.24$ on clean inputs and $0.54$--$0.58$ on moderate and heavy noise. To test path selection and threshold selection jointly, the $10$-pass path ($k=2$) is constructed to be slightly unsafe at every threshold ($\min_\lambda R(2,\lambda)=0.0629>0.05$), whereas the $13$-pass path ($k=4$) is safe up to $\lambda=0.50$ ($R(4,0.50)=0.0383$, $R(4,0.55)>0.05$) and the $16$-pass path ($k=6$) is safe up to $\lambda=0.55$ ($R(6,0.50)=0.0283$; Figure~\ref{fig:app-sim}). Population risks $R(k,\lambda)$ are evaluated on $400{,}000$ draws, after which calibration is repeated $1{,}000$ times at each sample size $n\in\{50,75,100,200,400\}$ with $\alpha=0.05$ and $\delta=0.10$ ($\delta/3=0.0333$ per path).

\subsection{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:app-sim} summarizes the $1{,}000$ calibration repetitions per sample size:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Exact finite-sample validity and the $n\ge 67$ floor.} At $n=50<67$, Learn-Then-Test certifies no candidate ($100\%$ safe fallback to the 21-pass baseline), confirming \Cref{eq:zerofail}. Across $n\in\{75,100,200,400\}$, Learn-Then-Test satisfies $R(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)\le 0.05$ in $97.4\%$ to $99.8\%$ of repetitions---well above the $90\%$ union-bound guarantee---and rejects the unsafe $10$-pass path ($k=2$) in $\ge 98.9\%$ of repetitions. By contrast, the naive rule picks the unsafe $k=2$ path in $25.4\%$--$52.4\%$ of repetitions and violates $\alpha=0.05$ in $59.9\%$--$79.9\%$ of runs ($\P(R\le\alpha)=0.201$--$0.401$).
\item \textbf{Sample size vs.\ path selection.} Because both $n=75$ and $n=100$ permit zero excess failures at level $\delta/3=0.0333$ (Table~\ref{tab:samplesize}), both are conservative ($44.6\%$ and $59.0\%$ fallback). At the study's planned $n=200$ (up to $4$ excess failures allowed), fallback drops to $5.2\%$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} selects the optimal $13$-pass path ($\hat k=4$) in $65.6\%$ of repetitions and $k=6$ ($16$ passes) in $28.8\%$ (mean cost $14.27$ passes, median $\hat\lambda_r=0.40$), and by $n=400$ it selects $\hat k=4$ in $91.5\%$ of runs ($13.26$ passes).
\end{enumerate}

\section[APPENDIX C.\ PROTOTYPE]{Prototype}
\label{app:prototype}

The study's prototype (\nolinkurl{code/scheduling/prototype/}) is an inference-time module and local demonstration rather than an end-user clinical application: it wraps the released DMOSpeech~2 networks with the payload router of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} without altering any model weights. Figure~\ref{fig:glance} summarizes what the prototype changes relative to the released sampler. Every utterance runs the same shortened teacher path and student schedule, while the router decides per utterance \emph{what} crosses at the handover---passing the teacher's own estimate $\hat x_1$ when safe, or replacing each band's value distribution with that of the clean student draft via $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when prompt noise or a large residual $r$ is detected.

To demonstrate the routing mechanism before the confirmatory calibration split is touched, the current prototype (\texttt{snr\_invariant.py}, \texttt{engine.py}, \texttt{server.py}, and \texttt{cli.py} in \nolinkurl{code/scheduling/prototype/}) executes the midpoint four-step path ($k=4$, $t_4=0.2929$, $j=2$, $13$ forward passes) on a central processing unit using exploratory development presets ($\lambda_r=0.342$ and $0.382$ from the feasibility pilot of Appendix~\ref{app:snr-r}, rather than the final $(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)$ that Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} will select on the calibration split). For any selected development speaker or uploaded prompt, the web interface streams the one-pass student draft $s_0$, the guided 8-interval teacher steps to $t_4$, the prompt SNR $\hat S$ against $\theta=20$~dB, the per-band invariant residual $r$, the chosen payload ($\hat x_1$ vs.\ $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$), and optional side-by-side synthesis of the 21-pass released system from identical seeds. \section[APPENDIX D.\ PRELIMINARY EXPERIMENTS]{Preliminary Experiments}
\label{app:prelim}

This appendix reports the pre-proposal experiments conducted on a Google Colab Tesla T4 GPU to validate the research problem, examine all fixed switching values of the released DMOSpeech~2 baseline, test the earlier concept-defense stopping-time rule, and motivate each design choice in Chapter~\ref{sec:method}. Every test used the automatic evaluation models of Section~\ref{sec:measures} (\texttt{Whisper-large-v3}, \texttt{WavLM-Base+}, \texttt{UTMOS}, and \texttt{pYIN}). Because speakers from the initial 500-speaker pool were inspected before the confirmatory protocol was locked, all numbers in this appendix serve strictly as preliminary problem-validation and design evidence rather than confirmatory proof. Table~\ref{tab:prelim} summarizes the five preliminary tests and how each shaped Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.

In the earlier concept-defense proposal, adaptation was framed as choosing \emph{when} the teacher stopped per utterance on the released 16-interval sway grid ($j=1$, $3$ student passes, $k\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}$, costing $13$--$21$ passes). That rule measured consecutive endpoint change with the endpoint-stabilization score
\begin{equation}
d_i = \frac{\|\hat x_1(t_i) - \hat x_1(t_{i-1})\|_M}{\|\hat x_1(t_i)\|_M + \epsilon},
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
\eqentry{Endpoint-stabilization score (earlier proposal)}
\begin{whereterms}[2.6cm]
$d_i$ & endpoint-stabilization score at 16-grid teacher step $i$; small means settled \\
$\hat x_1(t_i)$ & endpoint estimate at step $i$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}) on the 16-interval grid \\
$\|\cdot\|_M$ & Euclidean norm over the generated frames \\
$\epsilon$ & small positive constant against division by zero
\end{whereterms}
and stopped at the earliest candidate step where $d_i\le\lambda$ and $\hat S\ge 20$~dB, falling back to Step~8 ($21$ passes) otherwise, judged under an earlier baseline-relative failure rule ($\mathrm{WER}>\max(12.5\%,\mathrm{WER}_{\text{ref}}+1\%)$ or $\mathrm{SIM}<\mathrm{SIM}_{\text{ref}}-0.02$, with the Step-8 baseline treated as failure-free).

\subsection{Pre-Experiment 3: Pitch Variety and Student Restart Alignment}
\label{app:prelim-variety}

Because the 4-pass student alone is fast and avoids copying prompt noise, multi-seed tests ($12$--$20$ random seeds per speaker on the Tesla T4) examined what the teacher actually contributes on clean prompts and how the student's re-noising level $\tau$ affects retained prosody:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Variety dilution under an unaligned $j=1$ restart (Table~\ref{tab:prelim-variety-steps}).} Under the released code's $j=1$ restart ($\tau_1=0.0761$, which replaces $92.4\%$ of the handover tensor with fresh Gaussian noise), clean $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ plateaued by Step~2--3, and the 21-pass Step-8 baseline kept only $60\%$ of the published $14+2$ setting's raw $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ gain over student-only generation and only $35\%$ $[9\%,53\%]$ of its time-aligned pitch-contour gain. Decoding intermediate teacher mel tensors confirmed that by Step~8 the teacher already matched the $14+2$ melody within one semitone on $82\%$ of voiced frames, but restarting at $\tau_1=0.0761$ washed retention down to $53\%$ (vs.\ $79\%$--$86\%$ when restarting at $\tau_2=0.2929$).
\item \textbf{Schedule-aligned restarts recover teacher variety (Table~\ref{tab:prelim-variety-r2}).} Modeling a teacher-committed prosodic mode $\pm m$ with log-odds $\ell_k$ across student re-noising $\phi=\tau m\tanh(\ell_k/2)+(1-\tau)\varepsilon'$ gives mode-survival probability $R_k = \Phi\big((A|\tanh(\ell_k/2)|-\beta)/B\big)$ with $A=2m^2\tau^2/(1-\tau)^2$ and $B=2m\tau/(1-\tau)$. Raising $\tau$ from $0.0761$ ($j=1$) to $0.2929$ ($j=2$) multiplies $A/B$ by $\approx 5\times$. On $40$ fresh development speakers, matching $\tau=0.2929$ at $t_8=0.2929$ ($20$ passes) recovered pure-teacher pitch variety ($+0.0121$ $[+0.0081,+0.0159]$ over $j=1$, $p=8.36\times 10^{-6}$), whereas applying $j=2$ at an earlier mismatched stop ($t_4^{(16)}=0.0761\ne 0.2929$, $12$ passes) lowered clean UTMOS by $-0.373$. Aligning the student's restart point $\tau_{k/2}$ with the teacher's commit point $t_k$ on the 8-interval grid avoids this mismatch and defines Chapter~\ref{sec:method}'s candidate paths $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$ (\Cref{eq:paths}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pre-Experiment 4: Limitations of the Earlier Stopping-Time Rule Under Moderate Noise}
\label{app:prelim-cv}
\label{app:prelim-dev}

A fourth set of tests evaluated the earlier concept-defense stopping-time scheduler (\Cref{eq:stab}). When tested on clean and heavy $15$~dB white-noise prompts alone, the scheduler initially appeared effective: 4-fold cross-validation on $100$ development speakers ($200$ instances) averaged $18.88$ passes ($-10.1\%$) with $1/200$ relative failure, live early-exit timing on $200$ speakers on the Tesla T4 (Table~\ref{tab:cv-runtime}) confirmed proportional wall-clock speedups ($3.29$~s down to $2.40$~s at $\lambda=0.50$ and $2.06$~s at $13.08$ passes, with zero gate overhead on noisy prompts), and two-condition calibration accepted every threshold simply because $\theta=20$~dB blocked all $15$~dB prompts ($16.8$--$18.2$~dB).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{SNR gate leakage on moderate and band-limited noise.} White noise at $18$--$22$~dB produced $\hat S\in[19.0,24.8]$~dB, leaking past $\theta=20$~dB ($86/360 = 23.9\%$ relative failures), while telephone bandpass filtering silenced pause frames so $\hat S$ averaged $44.2$~dB and $20$~dB babble averaged $23.6$~dB.
\item \textbf{Premature stabilization of $d_i$ under noise.} By the Euler step and \Cref{eq:endpoint}, $\hat x_1(t_i)-\hat x_1(t_{i-1})=(1-t_i)(v_i-v_{i-1})$, so $d_i$ (\Cref{eq:stab}) measures Euler's local truncation error rather than prompt contamination. Acoustic noise weakened classifier-free guidance contrast ($\rho=0.89$ with $\|v_c-v_u\|$), dropping median $d_4$ from $0.61$ (clean) to $0.17$ ($15$~dB noise). Stopping when $d_i\le\lambda$ therefore exited \emph{earliest} on noisy prompts, and 5-fold Learn-Then-Test ($\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$) certified \textbf{no} stopping threshold ($0.0\%$ saving, $21.00$ passes, bottom rows of Table~\ref{tab:t4-steps1to8-policies}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pre-Experiment 5: Exploratory Handover Mechanisms Leading to Quantile Projection}
\label{app:drift-plan}

Follow-up exploratory tests on development speakers investigated how to preserve teacher prosody on a cheaper schedule while preventing copied prompt noise from reaching the student:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Trajectory extrapolation vs.\ per-band normalization:} Extrapolating teacher velocities across steps raised clean failures, whereas replacing each frequency band's mean energy with that of a one-step student draft cut white-noise failures from $126$ to $74/300$ ($p=2.3\times 10^{-6}$) with zero added clean failures, pointing toward per-band distribution matching ($G_1\subset G_2\subset G_3$ in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).
\item \textbf{Per-band quantile projection and coarse 8-interval teacher paths:} Mapping each band's sorted values onto the student draft's sorted values ($Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$, $G_3$ in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}) removed stationary background hiss while keeping the teacher's temporal rank structure ($64\%$ of aligned variety gain retained on noisy prompts). Separately, running the teacher on the 8-interval grid to $t_4=0.2929$ with $j=2$ ($12$ passes, $1.84$~s) retained $94\%$ ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$) and $97\%$ (aligned) of the published $30$-pass configuration's clean variety gain ($4.59$~s), but still produced $7/30$ runaway outputs under $15$~dB white noise unless paired with a clean payload route.
\end{itemize}
Together, these five preliminary tests established the two principles formalized in Section~\ref{sec:obj2}: (1)~stationary background noise lifts the teacher's mel distribution relative to the student draft $s_0$, producing a large 1D Wasserstein residual $r(\hat x_1,s_0)$ (\Cref{eq:w2}) even when $\hat S\ge 20$~dB; and (2)~routing between $\hat x_1$ and $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ on a shortened 8-interval candidate path ($k\in\{2,4,6\}$, selected once offline with Learn-Then-Test) achieves a fixed, predictable pass reduction while protecting speech quality per utterance.

\section[APPENDIX E.\ EXPLORATORY FEASIBILITY PILOT OF THE PAYLOAD ROUTER]{Exploratory Feasibility Pilot of the Payload Router}
\label{app:snr-r}

Before committing GPU budget to the full $200$-speaker multi-path calibration and $200$-speaker held-out evaluation proposed in Chapter~\ref{sec:method}, a pre-proposal feasibility pilot was conducted on a Google Colab Tesla T4 GPU using $30$ Seed-TTS development speakers (positions $31$--$60$) on the midpoint four-step candidate path ($k=4$, $t_4=0.2929$, $j=2$, $13$ forward passes). Its specific purpose was to test whether the invariant residual $r(\hat x_1,s_0)$ detects moderate $25$~dB background noise that passes the $20$~dB prompt-SNR gate, improves speech quality over single-signal rules, and preserves clean prosodic variety. The mathematical definitions and proofs of the condition-invariance ladder ($G_1\subset G_2\subset G_3$), quantile projection $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$, and invariant residual $r(\hat x_1,s_0)$ appear in Section~\ref{sec:obj2} (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}, Definitions~\ref{def:quantile}--\ref{def:policy}, and Propositions~\ref{prop:invariance}--\ref{prop:w2}). Because this pilot used $30$ development speakers and a single pre-set threshold ($\lambda_r=0.3400$ at $\theta=20$~dB) without running Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} across $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$, all results below are exploratory development evidence rather than confirmatory validation.

\subsection{Pilot Protocol and Evaluated Routing Rules}
\label{app:snr-r-verdict}

The pilot executed three stages on the Tesla T4 under a protocol frozen prior to synthesis: (1)~paired speech quality across all eight prompt conditions of Table~\ref{tab:conditions} (clean; white $15$~dB; white, pink, and high-pass noise at $25$~dB; reverberation; telephone; and babble at $15$~dB; $30\times 8 = 240$ utterances, including $20$ development speakers at positions $41$--$60$ not used in any earlier quality test); (2)~multi-seed pitch variety on $20$ speakers $\times 12$ seeds under clean and white $25$~dB prompts ($40$ blocks); and (3)~synchronized Tesla T4 wall-clock timing on $10$ clean utterances. All pre-experiment numerical checks of Section~\ref{sec:tools} passed with $0.0$ discrepancy and exact forward-pass counts. At commit point $t_4=0.2929$ ($j=2$), four routing rules were compared on identical seeds against the unrouted payloads (never projecting $\hat x_1$, $12$ passes; always projecting $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$, $13$ passes), the released 21-pass system, the published $14+2$ setting ($30$ passes), the student alone ($4$ passes), and the student restarted from its draft ($3$ passes):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{SNR-only router} ($12$--$13$ passes): pass $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when $\hat S < 20$~dB, else $\hat x_1$.
\item \emph{SNR + stabilization router} ($12$--$13$ passes): pass $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when $\hat S < 20$~dB or 8-grid stabilization $d_4^{(8)} < 0.2252$ (oriented in the low-drift noise direction).
\item \emph{Payload router ($\hat S$ and $r$)} ($13$ passes fixed): pass $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when $\hat S < 20$~dB or $r > 0.3400$, else $\hat x_1$.
\item \emph{Residual-only router} ($13$ passes fixed): pass $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when $r > 0.3400$, without the prompt-SNR gate.
\end{itemize}

Three diagnostic observations from this pilot directly shaped the confirmatory evaluation plan of Section~\ref{sec:obj4}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Ablations on the selected path.} Because the residual-only router and unconditional projection ($Q$ only) closely approached the two-signal payload router in mean quality ($2.949$ and $2.963$ vs.\ $2.950$ UTMOS, with the router's advantage over always projecting lying in $96\%$ vs.\ $87\%$ aligned clean pitch variety), Table~\ref{tab:systems} includes always projecting, never projecting, and the SNR-only router as explicit ablations on the selected path $\hat k$.
\item \textbf{Pre-specified variety test against the student from draft.} Restarting the student from its own draft without any teacher ($3$ passes, $0.511$~s) achieved the highest overall UTMOS ($3.114$) and lowest WER ($0.0310$) across degraded conditions, but lost clean pitch variety ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=0.0482$, aligned $0.0390$). Section~\ref{sec:stats} therefore fixes the paired $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ comparison between the router and the student from draft as the primary test of whether keeping teacher steps is justified.
\item \textbf{Channel limits and remaining confirmatory work.} All evaluated systems degraded under heavy room reverberation ($1.40$--$1.43$ UTMOS) and narrowband telephone filtering ($1.49$--$1.61$ UTMOS), where both $\hat x_1$ and $s_0$ inherit convolutional channel coloration (Section~\ref{sec:condition-model}). Finally, because this feasibility pilot tested only $k=4$ on $30$ development speakers without statistical path selection, evaluating all three candidate paths $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$, calibrating $(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)$ on the $200$-speaker calibration split via Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt}, and testing on the $200$ held-out Seed-TTS speakers and LibriSpeech-PC remain the confirmatory tasks of Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.
\end{enumerate}
