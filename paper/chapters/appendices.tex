\section[APPENDIX A.\ SAMPLE CALCULATIONS]{Sample Calculations}
\label{app:calc}

This appendix works through the formulas of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} on recorded values from the team's runs on Google Colab Tesla T4 GPUs (\nolinkurl{research/evidence/benchmarks/gpu_grid_all1000.csv} and the \texttt{EXP-011} pilot \nolinkurl{worked_example.json}), using the development split only. Sections~\ref{app:calc-grid} to~\ref{app:calc-fail} follow one development utterance through the stopping policy with $\lambda=0.50$ and $\theta=20$~dB: \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901} (target text ``We don't have the budget to produce it in studio quality.''), chosen as the first development utterance in ID order that this policy keeps past Step~4. Section~\ref{app:calc-ltt} then computes the fixed-sequence Learn-Then-Test p-values and sample-size limit on the 100 development speakers. Notice that the initial T4 development grid and \texttt{EXP-011} recorded Steps~$\{4,5,6,8\}$, whereas candidate Step~7 ($s=0.20$, 19 passes; Table~\ref{tab:app-grid}) was added afterwards and benchmarked on the Tesla T4 in \texttt{EXP-018} (Table~\ref{tab:prelim-steps1to7}).

\subsection{Prompt SNR Estimate}

In the \texttt{EXP-011} Tesla T4 recording of \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901}, the prompt spans 459 frames (25~ms window, 10~ms hop), so the quietest 10\% is 46 frames and the loudest half is 230 frames. Under the clean prompt, $\bar E_{\text{bottom}} = 1.747\times 10^{-5}$ and $\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.043\times 10^{-2}$; by \Cref{eq:snr},
\[
\hat S = 10\log_{10}\frac{0.02043}{1.747\times 10^{-5}} = 10\log_{10}1169.4 = 30.68\ \text{dB}\ (\text{30.7~dB in the T4 grid}).
\]
Since $30.7\ge\theta=20$, the SNR gate is open and early stopping is allowed (Algorithm~\ref{alg:handover}, line~1). Under the same utterance's \texttt{noisy15} prompt (15~dB white noise added), $\bar E_{\text{bottom}} = 3.563\times 10^{-4}$ and $\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.076\times 10^{-2}$, giving $\hat S = 10\log_{10}(0.02076/3.563\times 10^{-4}) = 17.65\ \text{dB}$ ($17.7$~dB $<20$), so the gate closes and holds the teacher until Step~8.

\subsection{Endpoint Estimate}

At step 5 ($t_5=0.1181$) on the clean prompt of \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901}, the \texttt{EXP-011} T4 trace records at mel bin 50 of generated frame 10 the state entry $x_{t_5}=-0.6905$ and guided velocity $v=-0.5198$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:baseline}). By \Cref{eq:endpoint}, that entry's endpoint estimate is
\[
\hat x_1(t_5) = -0.6905 + (1-0.1181)\times(-0.5198) = -0.6905 - 0.4584 = -1.1489.
\]
The same operation is applied across every entry of the generated mel-spectrogram. The velocity is computed anyway for the Euler step, so this estimate costs no forward pass (Proposition~\ref{prop:zero}).

\subsection{Endpoint-Stabilization Score and Stopping Decision}

Across all generated frames of \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901} on the Tesla T4 (\texttt{EXP-011} / \texttt{gpu\_grid\_all1000.csv}) using \Cref{eq:stab} with $\lambda=0.50$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Step 4 (first candidate, clean): the change norm is $\|\hat x_1(t_4)-\hat x_1(t_3)\|_M = 116.17$ and the estimate norm is $\|\hat x_1(t_4)\|_M = 218.01$, so $d_4 = 116.17/218.01 = 0.533 > \lambda = 0.50$. By \Cref{eq:policy} the teacher continues (Algorithm~\ref{alg:handover}, line~7).
\item Step 5 (clean): the change norm falls to $85.92$ and the estimate norm is $204.42$, so $d_5 = 85.92/204.42 = 0.420 \le 0.50$, and $\hat S=30.7\ge20$. The teacher stops at step 5 (lines~4--5). (For reference, $d_6 = 65.97/197.16 = 0.335$.)
\end{itemize}
By \Cref{eq:cost} the clean utterance costs $2(5+1)+3 = 15$ forward passes instead of 21, saving $6/21 = 28.6\%$. Under its \texttt{noisy15} prompt, by contrast, Step~4 already shows $d_4 = 42.93/295.32 = 0.145 \le 0.50$ ($d_5=0.135$, $d_6=0.116$), illustrating how noise makes the score drop prematurely (Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-dev}); the SNR gate ($\hat S=17.7<20$~dB) therefore keeps the teacher running to Step~8 at 21 passes.

\subsection{Added-Failure Check}
\label{app:calc-fail}

With \Cref{eq:failure} and $c=12.5\%$, $m_W=1$ point, $m_S=0.02$ on the Tesla T4 runs (\nolinkurl{gpu_grid_all1000.csv}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No failure.} Development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901} (clean) at its chosen Step~5: WER 0\%, SIM 0.963; Step-8 reference: WER 0\%, SIM 0.966. The WER limit is $\max(12.5\%,\,0\%+1\%)=12.5\%$ and the SIM limit is $0.966-0.02=0.946$; both are respected, so $f=0$.
\item \emph{Failure.} Development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_15734837-common_voice_en_15734838} with its \texttt{noisy15} prompt at Step~4 (which the SNR gate blocks at $\hat S=17.5$~dB): WER 37.50\% (3 errors in 8 normalized words), SIM 0.889; Step-8 reference: WER 0\%, SIM 0.916. Both the WER limit ($12.5\%$) and the SIM limit ($0.916-0.02=0.896$) are missed, so $f=1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibration p-Values and Sample Size}
\label{app:calc-ltt}

To illustrate Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} on real T4 counts without touching the calibration split, take the $n=100$ development speakers from \nolinkurl{gpu_grid_all1000.csv} with $\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$, SNR gate $\theta=20$~dB, and the full 21-value grid $\Lambda=\{0.20,0.25,\dots,1.20\}$ of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} (counting a speaker as failing when either its clean or its \texttt{noisy15} case fails). Table~\ref{tab:app-ltt} lists the observed failure counts and binomial p-values from \Cref{eq:pvalue}; for example, with one failure,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p &= \P\big(\mathrm{Bin}(100,0.05)\le1\big) = 0.95^{100} + 100\times0.05\times0.95^{99}\\
  &= 0.0059 + 0.0312 = 0.0371,
\end{aligned}
\]
whereas two failures yield $p=\P\big(\mathrm{Bin}(100,0.05)\le2\big)=0.1183 > 0.10$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} certifies $\lambda=0.20$ through $\lambda=0.50$, stops at $\lambda=0.55$, and returns $\hat\lambda=0.50$. By \Cref{eq:guarantee}, on an independent calibration split of $n$ speakers this guarantees $R(\pi_{\hat\lambda})\le 5\%$ with probability at least 90\%. (Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-cv} reports what actually occurred when Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} ran on the 200 calibration speakers of \nolinkurl{gpu_grid_all1000.csv}, where every threshold up to $\hat\lambda=1.20$ was certified, and across multi-noise T4 runs in \texttt{EXP-012}/\texttt{EXP-017}, where premature stabilization kept all thresholds uncertified.)

\emph{Smallest calibration set with zero failures.} With no failures, \Cref{eq:pvalue} gives $p=(1-\alpha)^n$. Certifying $\alpha=0.05$ at $\delta=0.10$ needs $0.95^n\le0.10$, i.e.\ $n\ge\ln0.10/\ln0.95 = 44.9$. Indeed $0.95^{44}=0.105$ fails and $0.95^{45}=0.099$ passes, so at least 45 independent inputs are needed. With 36 inputs, $p=0.95^{36}=0.158>0.10$, which is why an earlier sub-45 sample could not certify this tolerance.

\section[APPENDIX B.\ SAMPLE SIMULATION]{Sample Simulation}
\label{app:sim}

This appendix checks, on simulated data, whether the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:obj3} keeps its promise $\P(R\le\alpha)\ge1-\delta$, and compares it with a naive rule that picks the most aggressive threshold whose observed failure rate is at most $\alpha$. No speech is synthesized; the data are random numbers with the structure of Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Setup}

Each simulated utterance has a prompt SNR, stabilization scores $d_4,d_5,d_6$ at the three candidate steps, and an added-failure indicator for each candidate. A quarter of the utterances are noisy, with $\hat S\sim\mathcal N(16,2^2)$~dB; the rest are clean, with $\hat S\sim\mathcal N(33,4^2)$~dB. An utterance difficulty $h\sim\mathrm{LogNormal}(0,0.45^2)$ drives the scores, which shrink at later steps:
\[
d_k = h\, e^{-0.35(k-4)}\, u_k,\qquad u_k\sim\mathrm{LogNormal}(-0.55,\,0.25^2),\qquad k\in\{4,5,6\}.
\]
A candidate fails with a probability that grows with its score and is higher on noisy prompts:
\[
\P\big(f(x,k)=1\big) = \sigma\big(-4.4 + 3.2\,d_k + 1.6\,\mathbb 1[\text{noisy}] - 0.45\,(k-4)\big),
\]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function; the reference never fails, as in Definition~\ref{def:failure}. The policy of \Cref{eq:policy} uses $\theta=20$~dB and the grid $\lambda\in\{0.20,0.25,\dots,1.20\}$, with costs from \Cref{eq:cost}. The true risk of every threshold is computed on 400{,}000 simulated utterances (Figure~\ref{fig:app-sim}). Calibration is then repeated 1{,}000 times for each calibration size $n$, with $\alpha=0.05$ and $\delta=0.10$, and the true risk of each returned threshold is recorded. The script \nolinkurl{code/analysis/ltt_sim.py} runs both this synthetic stress test (chosen so thresholds above $0.85$ violate $\alpha=0.05$) and the same model fitted directly to the Colab Tesla T4 development split (\texttt{-{}-fitted} below). Note that \nolinkurl{gpu_grid_all1000.csv} evaluates candidates $\{4,5,6\}$ and Step~8, so both simulation regimes use those three candidates.

\subsection{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:app-sim} summarizes the 1{,}000 repetitions. Learn-Then-Test met its promise at every calibration size: the returned threshold had true risk at most 5\% in 92.0\% to 98.8\% of repetitions, above the required 90\%. The naive rule did not: because it picks the threshold that happens to look best on one sample, its threshold exceeded the tolerance in more than half of the repetitions, even though its average risk was close to 5\%.

The price of the guarantee is computation. With 50 calibration inputs, Learn-Then-Test was conservative, certified no threshold in about one repetition in ten, and used 18.2 forward passes on average; with 400 inputs it used 15.9, close to the 15.4 of the largest safe threshold. This is the trade-off that the calibration-size analysis of Objective~3 (Section~\ref{sec:obj4}) will measure on real speech.

The simulation assumes independent inputs from one distribution, exactly the condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}. It therefore shows that the procedure is implemented and behaves as the theory says when unsafe thresholds exist, not that the condition holds for real speech; that is what the held-out and repeated-split checks of Section~\ref{sec:obj3} will test.

\emph{Model fitted to the Colab Tesla T4 development data.} Running the same simulation with parameters fitted to the 100 development speakers' Tesla T4 runs (\texttt{python code/analysis/ltt\_sim.py -{}-fitted}, reading \nolinkurl{gpu_grid_all1000.csv}) produces equal clean and \texttt{noisy15} proportions; prompt SNR $37.9\pm7.5$~dB (clean) vs.\ $17.8\pm0.3$~dB (\texttt{noisy15}); lower stabilization scores on noisy prompts ($\ln d_4 = -1.739\pm0.198$ vs.\ $-0.542\pm0.217$ clean); and a fitted failure logit $(-2.640 - 2.504\,d_k + 3.583\,\mathbb{1}[\text{noisy}] - 0.350\,(k-4))$, which rises steeply with prompt noise but decreases with $d_k$ ($-2.504$). Because the 20~dB gate sends every \texttt{noisy15} prompt to Step~8, true risk stays at most $0.0089$ ($<1\%$) across the entire grid (largest safe threshold $1.20$, risk $0.0080$, $17.05$ passes). Over 1{,}000 repetitions at every $n\in\{50,100,200,400\}$, both Learn-Then-Test and the naive rule stay below $\alpha=0.05$ in $100.0\%$ of repetitions; Learn-Then-Test is simply more cautious at smaller $n$, progressing from median $\hat\lambda=0.40$ ($18.96$ passes, $8.6\%$ reference-only) at $n=50$ and $\hat\lambda=0.60$ ($18.19$ passes, $1.3\%$ reference-only) at $n=100$ to $\hat\lambda=1.20$ ($17.08$ passes, $0.0\%$ reference-only) at $n=200$ (matching the 200-speaker calibration T4 outcome in Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-cv}, whereas adding intermediate noise changes the behavior as documented in Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-cv}--\ref{app:prelim-dev}).

\section[APPENDIX C.\ PROTOTYPE]{Prototype}
\label{app:prototype}

The study's prototype is an inference method rather than a user-facing application: the released DMOSpeech~2 sampler with the stopping controller of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} added. Figure~\ref{fig:glance} shows what the prototype changes for a user. The same networks produce the speech; only the point where the teacher hands over to the student moves, separately for each input, and that point never moves later than in the baseline.

A working demonstration already exists: a local prototype (\texttt{code/\allowbreak prototype}) runs the released DMOSpeech~2 on a central processing unit with the SNR gate and the stabilization rule of \Cref{eq:policy}. For one input it shows the prompt SNR against the gate, each teacher step with its stabilization score as it is computed, the step at which the rule hands over, the student's steps, and the forward passes used against the 21 of the reference, and it can also synthesize the reference output from the same trajectory and seeds for listening. Table~\ref{tab:samples} gives sample inputs. The prototype is a demonstration, not an evaluation tool: its thresholds are development values, not the calibrated $\hat\lambda$.

For the experiments, the same controller will be packaged as one module shared by all experiment scripts (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). It takes the prompt audio, the prompt text, the target text, the calibrated threshold $\hat\lambda$ and the SNR gate $\theta$, and returns the waveform together with the chosen stopping step, the number of forward passes used, the prompt SNR estimate and the trace of stabilization scores. Before any experiment the module will pass the three checks of Section~\ref{sec:setup}: identical outputs for repeated runs with the same seed, exact reproduction of the reference when the controller falls back, and forward-pass counts equal to \Cref{eq:cost}.

The three boxes at the bottom of Figure~\ref{fig:glance} correspond to what the study must establish about the handover point: how it is decided (Objectives~1 and~2), how safe the decision is (Objectives~2 and~3, Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}), and that it never costs more than the baseline (Proposition~\ref{prop:fallback}).

\section[APPENDIX D.\ PRELIMINARY EXPERIMENTS]{Preliminary Experiments}
\label{app:prelim}

The team's preliminary and post-proposal development experiments are reported here as development evidence. Every benchmark, calibration replay, timing check, and diversity measurement in this appendix was executed on Google Colab Tesla T4 GPUs with the evaluation models of Section~\ref{sec:measures} (\texttt{Whisper-large-v3}, \texttt{WavLM-Base+}, \texttt{UTMOS}, and \texttt{pYIN}); only Colab T4 experiments are included so that all appendices rest uniformly on GPU evidence. The initial 500-speaker T4 benchmarks used the three early candidate steps of the time (teacher steps 4, 5 and 6) and the Step-8 reference; teacher step 7 ($s=0.20$) was added to the candidate set afterwards and benchmarked on the Tesla T4 alongside earlier checkpoints (\texttt{Steps~1--7}) in \texttt{EXP-003}, \texttt{EXP-017}, and \texttt{EXP-018}, followed by multi-seed variety and handover probes (\texttt{EXP-020}, \texttt{EXP-022}, \texttt{EXP-026}--\texttt{EXP-033}). They motivated the architecture of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} and fixed several of its design settings, but they remain development evidence rather than independent held-out proof. Table~\ref{tab:prelim} summarizes what each Colab T4 experiment found and what it changed in the study.

The stopping-time scheduler studied in these experiments stopped the teacher at the first candidate step at which its estimate of the final output had settled. It measured this with the endpoint-stabilization score
\begin{equation}
d_i = \frac{\|\hat x_1(t_i) - \hat x_1(t_{i-1})\|_M}{\|\hat x_1(t_i)\|_M + \epsilon},
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
\eqentry{Endpoint-stabilization score (earlier proposal)}
\begin{whereterms}[2.6cm]
$d_i$ & endpoint-stabilization score at teacher step $i$; small means settled \\
$\hat x_1(t_i)$ & endpoint estimate at step $i$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}) on the 16-interval grid \\
$\|\cdot\|_M$ & Euclidean norm over the generated frames \\
$\epsilon$ & small positive constant that prevents division by zero
\end{whereterms}
and stopped at step $i$ when $d_i\le\lambda$ and the prompt SNR estimate (\Cref{eq:snr}) was at least $\theta=20$~dB, falling back to step 8 otherwise.

\subsection{Fixed Switching Times}

This analysis used all 500 speakers of the three splits (Section~\ref{sec:data}), each under the clean and the 15~dB noisy prompt condition (1{,}000 instances), synthesized on the Tesla T4 GPU with the student restart fixed, evaluated with the models of Section~\ref{sec:measures}, and scored with the added-failure rule of Definition~\ref{def:failure} against the reference switching time $s=0.25$ (Step~8; \nolinkurl{code/analysis/problem_validation_t4.py}). With one switching time for every input, the failure rate rose as the switch moved earlier, and almost all failures came from noisy prompts (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-fail}); the same ordering held on each split separately. The cheapest switching time that did not fail varied between inputs (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-oracle}), and the 179 instances that needed the reference were all noisy. That noisy audio prompts degrade flow-matching zero-shot synthesis has been reported before \parencite{wang2024noise}. A per-input choice made in hindsight would therefore use 14.85 forward passes on average instead of 21 ($-29\%$), whereas no single earlier switching time is safe for every input.

\subsection{Student Restart Level and Paper Baselines}

Restarting the student one grid point later ($j=2$, two student steps, the configuration reported in the DMOSpeech~2 paper \parencite{li2025dmospeech2}) saves one forward pass relative to $j=1$ ($\tau=0.0761$, three student steps). Tested on the Tesla T4 across $360$ benchmark utterances spanning six prompt conditions (\texttt{EXP-017}), the paper's $14+2$ configuration ($j=2$, $30$ NFE) incurred $140$ of $360$ ($38.9\%$) added failures against the 16-grid Step-8 reference ($34.17\%$ noisy WER vs.\ $24.16\%$ for Step~4, or $7.61\%$ for Step~8 in \texttt{EXP-018}), and exact Algorithm~2 ($32$ NFE) incurred $172$ of $360$ ($47.8\%$) added failures ($41.01\%$ noisy WER), whereas matching $s=0.25$ on the 32-interval grid with $j=1$ ($33$ NFE) matched 16-grid Step~8 in continuous quality ($6.84\%$ vs.\ $6.79\%$ WER; $0.810$ vs.\ $0.810$ SIM) at $+57\%$ higher runtime ($5.03$~s vs.\ $3.23$~s). Meanwhile, 32-step teacher-only F5-TTS \parencite{chen2024f5} required $64$ NFE ($9.65$~s) and suffered $97.36\%$ WER on noisy prompts due to unregularized classifier-free guidance noise amplification. The restart level in Chapter~\ref{sec:method} is therefore fixed at $j=1$ ($\tau=0.0761$, three student passes) as a controlled setting, and the teacher-to-student switching time is the sole handover variable varied (while Section~\ref{app:prelim-variety} documents how $j=1$ vs.\ $j=2$ affects multi-seed clean prosodic variety, and Figure~\ref{fig:baseline-handover} in Appendix~\ref{app:snr-r-arch} diagrams the released static baseline handover pipeline).

\subsection{Prosodic Variety Across Switching Steps and Restart Levels}
\label{app:prelim-variety}

Although Chapter~\ref{sec:method} evaluates intelligibility, speaker similarity, predicted naturalness, and cost without using output diversity as a primary criterion (Section~\ref{sec:rrl-distill}), teacher-guided sampling in DMOSpeech~2 was introduced specifically to counteract mode shrinkage in the distilled student \parencite{li2025dmospeech2}. Ten post-proposal Tesla T4 development runs (\texttt{EXP-020}, \texttt{EXP-022}, \texttt{EXP-023}, \texttt{EXP-026}--\texttt{EXP-032}) therefore measured multi-seed prosodic variety across $12$ or $20$ latent seeds per clean prompt via pYIN fundamental-frequency variation: unaligned frame-wise pitch coefficient of variation ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$), time-aligned pitch variation ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}^{\text{aligned}}$, after warping each seed to seed~1 so timing shifts do not inflate pitch variance), and inter-seed timing spread in mel frames. Selective band ablations at Step~4 (\texttt{EXP-020}, $n=20\times 20$ seeds) showed that the slow prosody component ($\approx 96$~ms moving average) carries pitch diversity ($\Delta\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=-0.0402$ $[-0.0589,-0.0249]$ when replaced by the student's draft), whereas swapping only the time-averaged channel voice band cuts noisy speaker-similarity failures without losing clean pitch variety.

While \texttt{W8} recovers $85\%$--$88\%$ of \texttt{P25}'s timing-spread gain and $60\%$ of its raw $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ gain, it retains only $35\%$ $[9\%, 53\%]$ of \texttt{P25}'s aligned pitch-contour gain (\texttt{P25} exceeds \texttt{W8} in $19$ of $20$ speakers), while scoring $+0.176$ $[+0.046,+0.348]$ higher clean UTMOS. \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Recovery at Step~8 (\texttt{W8R2}) vs.\ early $j=2$ stops (\texttt{W4R2}, \texttt{W5R2}, \texttt{W6R2}):} Restarting at $\tau=0.2929$ from Step~8 (\texttt{W8R2}, $20$ passes) recovers pure-teacher pitch variety (\texttt{T16}, $32$ passes: $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=0.1171$ vs.\ $0.1175$ in \texttt{EXP-026}) and significantly exceeds \texttt{W8} ($+0.0121$ $[+0.0081,+0.0159]$ in \texttt{EXP-027} on $34/40$ fresh speakers, $p=8.36\times 10^{-6}$; common-frame $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=+0.0107$), while matching \texttt{P25} in UTMOS on shared clean utterances ($+0.003$ $[-0.126,+0.112]$). Earlier $j=2$ stops were evaluated at Steps~4, 5, and~6 (Steps~1--3 and~7 under $j=2$ were not run): \texttt{W4R2} ($12$ passes) drops clean UTMOS by $-0.373$ (\texttt{EXP-027}) to $-0.393$ (\texttt{EXP-028} \texttt{unseen\_cal}), whereas \texttt{W6R2} ($16$ passes) retains $69\%$--$86\%$ of \texttt{W8R2}'s $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ gain over \texttt{W4} at comparable UTMOS to \texttt{W8R2} ($-0.063$ to $-0.095$ vs.\ \texttt{W8}).

Moreover, on \texttt{noisy15} prompts teacher-handed outputs copy the white noise (\texttt{UTMOS} $\approx 1.25$ in \texttt{EXP-029}/\texttt{EXP-032}), causing pYIN to voice $0\%$ of frames in at least half of all seeds for every teacher arm (\texttt{EXP-022}, \texttt{EXP-032}: $0/20$ speakers defined for \texttt{W1}--\texttt{W3} and \texttt{P25}, and $\le 1/20$ for \texttt{W4}, \texttt{W8}, and \texttt{P25R}) while $j=2$ stops incur more \texttt{noisy15} WER runaways than \texttt{W8} ($5$ for \texttt{W5R2}, $6$ for \texttt{W8R2}, $0$ for \texttt{W8} in \texttt{EXP-029}). Whether UTMOS reductions on clean expressive outputs reflect synthesis artifacts or scorer preference remains unverified without listening tests.
\end{enumerate}

\phead{Theoretical model behind $j=1$ vs.\ $j=2$ retention (handover transmissibility, \texttt{EXP-026})} To explain why restart level $\tau$ governs variety survival largely independently of teacher step $k\ge 3$, consider a one-dimensional prosodic axis with two modes at $\pm m$, where at teacher step $k$ the guided posterior log-odds for the dominant mode are $\ell_k$ (estimate $m\tanh(\ell_k/2)$) and the student re-noising step sets $\phi = \tau\,m\tanh(\ell_k/2)+(1-\tau)\,\varepsilon'$ with $\varepsilon'\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Modeling the distilled student as a mode-seeking classifier with bias $\beta > \log(\pi/(1-\pi))$ toward the dominant mode under distribution-matching distillation (\nolinkurl{docs/progress/log/2026-11-14-exp026-transmissibility-test.md}), a teacher-committed minority mode survives the restart with probability
\[
R_k = \Phi\!\left(\frac{A\,|\tanh(\ell_k/2)| - \beta}{B}\right), \qquad
R^\ast = \Phi\!\left(\frac{A-\beta}{B}\right),
\]
where $A = 2m^2\tau^2/(1-\tau)^2$ and $B = 2m\tau/(1-\tau)$. Raising $\tau$ from $0.0761$ ($j=1$) to $0.2929$ ($j=2$) multiplies $A$ by $\approx 25$ and $B$ by $\approx 5$ (and thus the teacher-to-restart signal ratio $A/B$ by $\approx 5\times$), predicting both why \texttt{W8} loses teacher melody to \texttt{P25}/\texttt{W8R2} even at Step~8 and why retention plateaus once the teacher commits ($\ell_k$ large) at a fixed restart level. While this simplified binary-mode model correctly predicted the direction of the $j=2$ recovery across \texttt{EXP-026}--\texttt{EXP-027}, continuous trade-off tests (\texttt{EXP-029}) showed that raising transmissibility moves monotonically along a clean $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$--UTMOS curve rather than creating a cost-free ceiling.

\subsection{Stabilization Policy: Cross-Validation, Runtime, and Multi-Noise Learn-Then-Test}
\label{app:prelim-cv}

Using the 100 development speakers on the Google Colab Tesla T4 GPU with the evaluation models of Section~\ref{sec:measures} under the clean and \texttt{noisy15} prompt conditions (200 instances; \texttt{EXP-009}, \nolinkurl{code/analysis/cv_stabilization_t4.py}), the policy of Definition~\ref{def:policy} with $\theta=20$~dB was first evaluated by four-fold speaker-grouped cross-validation across the early candidates $\{4,5,6,8\}$. In each fold the threshold $\lambda$ was chosen on 75 speakers (150 instances) as the largest value in $\{0.20,0.25,\dots,0.60\}$ with zero added failures and frozen before scoring the 25 held-out speakers. The folds selected $\lambda=0.35$, 0.35, 0.35 and 0.50; widening the search range to 1.20 leaves these four T4 fold thresholds unchanged. Out of fold, mean cost fell from 21 to 18.88 forward passes ($-10.1\%$) with one added failure in 200 instances, against 50 of 200 for a fixed $s=0.15$, and the SNR gate sent all 100 noisy prompts to the reference, so the saving came from the clean prompts. Tables~\ref{tab:cv-policies} and~\ref{tab:cv-quality} compare it on the same 200 instances with the methods of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} (Table~\ref{tab:methods}), measured and tested as planned there. Against every fixed early switching time the policy had far fewer added failures (exact McNemar $p<10^{-13}$ for each), and the upper end of its 95\% interval for the failure rate, 2.8\%, stayed below the 5\% tolerance. Against the reference it used significantly fewer forward passes (Wilcoxon signed-rank test on speaker-level values, Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-15}$), while its differences in WER, SIM and UTMOS were not significant (Holm-adjusted $p=1.00$ for each); a claim that its quality equals the reference's needs the two one-sided tests with margins fixed before the held-out test. On clean prompts it saved 20.2\% of the passes and chose Steps~4, 5 and~6 for 6, 28 and 52 inputs, so the choice adapted per input. The SNR-gated controls behaved similarly on these data: SNR-only, the SNR gate followed by Step~5 or Step~6, and a random assignment of the policy's own steps among the gated inputs (2{,}000 draws, mean 0.75 failures) all stayed within 0 to 3 failures, and none differed significantly from the policy (exact McNemar $p\ge0.50$). Stabilization alone, without the gate, found no threshold with zero training failures in any fold and kept every input at the reference, because noisy prompts stabilize early. With at most three failures per method, these data cannot separate the SNR-gated variants; the investigation plan of Appendix~\ref{app:drift-plan} is designed for that. Two limitations remain for this cross-validation step. Selecting a threshold with zero training failures is not a Learn-Then-Test certificate: with 75 training speakers and no failures, the smallest risk that can be certified at $\delta=0.10$ is about 3\% (Appendix~\ref{app:calc-ltt}); after zero observed failures the upper confidence limit shrinks only in proportion to $1/n$, about $3/n$ at 95\% confidence \parencite{hanley1983zero}. And the development split also served other design decisions, so these out-of-fold figures may be optimistic, because reusing data that guided earlier choices to select and then report performance is a known source of selection bias \parencite{cawley2010overfitting}. Objective~2 replaces this cross-validation heuristic with fixed-sequence Learn-Then-Test on speaker-disjoint data.

\emph{Verdict, Learn-Then-Test across two vs.\ six conditions, and all-checkpoint vs.\ policy comparison.} Taken together, the 200-instance T4 cross-validation (Tables~\ref{tab:cv-policies}--\ref{tab:cv-quality}) and \texttt{EXP-010} timing run supported per-input switching when prompts are restricted to clean vs.\ 15~dB noise (1/200 failure vs.\ 50--66/200 for fixed early steps, saving 10.1\% of passes overall and 27\% of clean synthesis time). However, auditing Chapter~3's Learn-Then-Test calibration directly on Tesla T4 data uncovered why those preliminary clean-vs-\texttt{noisy15} cross-validation results do not carry over unchanged to LTT:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Two-condition T4 benchmark (\texttt{AUDIT-001}, 200 calibration speakers, Steps $\{4,5,6,8\}$):} Because $\theta=20$~dB blocked all \texttt{noisy15} prompts ($16.8$--$18.2$~dB) and clean early stops rarely failed, every p-value on $\Lambda=\{0.20..1.20\}$ stayed $\le 0.0623\le\delta=0.10$, so LTT certified the grid maximum $\hat\lambda=1.20$ and reduced Definition~\ref{def:policy} to \texttt{SNR-only} ($17.04$ NFE, $4/200$ test failures).
\item \textbf{Six-condition T4 benchmarks (\texttt{EXP-012}, \texttt{EXP-017}, \texttt{EXP-018}, $n=360$ across 60 dev speakers $\times$ 6 conditions):} When intermediate white noise (18--25~dB) entered alongside clean and 15~dB noise, prompts in 18--22~dB crossed $\theta=20$~dB ($23.9\%$ failures for uncalibrated \texttt{SNR-only}), while noise lowered $d_i$ prematurely (Section~\ref{app:prelim-dev}). Consequently, under LTT (5-fold out-of-fold speaker CV, $\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$), Definition~\ref{def:policy} ($\theta=20$~dB) certified \emph{no} threshold on the 16-grid ($0.0\%$ saving, $21.00$ NFE in both \texttt{EXP-017} on $\{4,5,6,8\}$ and \texttt{EXP-018} across Steps~1--8), and saved only $1.8\%$ on the 32-grid. Over $\{4,5,6,8\}$ in \texttt{EXP-017}, calibrating the SNR gate itself ($\hat\theta=26$~dB) yielded $18.71$ NFE ($7/360 = 1.9\%$ failures, TOST $p_{\mathrm{WER}}=3\times 10^{-24}$), and pairing Objective~1's $28$~dB gate with $\hat\lambda=1.20$ gave $19.53$ NFE ($4/360 = 1.1\%$, $p_{\mathrm{WER}}=7\times 10^{-29}$).
\item \textbf{Binary noise floor ($22.5\%$):} Comparing the two equal-quality references directly in \texttt{EXP-017} (32-grid $s=0.25$ vs.\ 16-grid Step~8, $0.000$ mean SIM difference) still flagged $81$ of $360$ ($22.5\%$) binary ``added failures'' solely from stochastic student seed variance on noisy prompts, motivating paired continuous differences and TOST equivalence alongside binary counts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Development-Split Signal Diagnostic and Premature Stabilization}
\label{app:prelim-dev}

A first diagnostic of the two signals was run on the 100 development speakers on the Tesla T4 with the evaluation models of this study (Section~\ref{sec:measures}), each under the clean and the 15~dB noisy condition. On those two extreme conditions alone, the estimated prompt SNR separated the groups completely: 23.3 to 50.0~dB for clean prompts and 16.8 to 18.2~dB for 15~dB noisy prompts, so a 20~dB gate sent every 15~dB noisy prompt to the reference. Blind estimation of SNR from statistics of the waveform is established practice \parencite{kim2008wada}; the estimator of Definition~\ref{def:snr} uses a ratio of frame energies instead. However, when tested across intermediate and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) acoustic corruptions on the Tesla T4 (\texttt{EXP-011} and \texttt{EXP-012}), both initial signal assumptions broke down:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Prompt SNR failure on moderate and OOD noise.} Prompts degraded with 18 to 22~dB white Gaussian noise produced frame-energy estimates of 19.0 to 24.8~dB, crossing the $\theta=20$~dB gate and causing $86$ of $360$ ($23.9\%$) added failures---$4.8\times$ above the $5\%$ tolerance. On OOD corruptions (\texttt{EXP-011}, $n=100$), bandpass telephone filtering silenced pause frames so that the estimator reported a mean SNR of $44.2$~dB (``ultra-clean'') and prematurely exited on degraded speech, while 20~dB babble measured $23.6$~dB and waveform SNR estimation added $1.85$~ms of overhead per utterance.
\item \textbf{Premature stabilization of single-step drift.} By the Euler update (Algorithm~\ref{alg:baseline}) and endpoint formula (\Cref{eq:endpoint}), consecutive teacher endpoint estimates satisfy the exact algebraic identity $\hat x_1(t_i)-\hat x_1(t_{i-1})=(1-t_i)\bigl(v(x_{t_i},t_i)-v(x_{t_{i-1}},t_{i-1})\bigr)$, so $d_i$ (\Cref{eq:stab}) is proportional to Euler's local truncation error $\frac{h}{2}(v_i-v_{i-1})$ and tracks trajectory stiffness rather than prompt contamination. On clean prompts, single-step $d_4$ showed near-chance failure discrimination (ROC area $\approx 0.47$ from two to three failures). Across noise levels (\texttt{EXP-012}, $n=360$), acoustic noise weakened classifier-free guidance contrast (Spearman $\rho=0.89$ with the conditional--unconditional gap $v_c-v_u$, whose median fell from $0.236$ on clean prompts to $0.116$ at 15~dB), causing median $d_4$ to drop from $0.61$ (clean) to $0.17$ (15~dB noisy). Degraded trajectories therefore \emph{stabilize prematurely}: stopping when $d_i\le\lambda$ hands noisy prompts over first rather than last.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Plan for Further Investigation and Exploratory Candidates}
\label{app:drift-plan}

Because the value of the single-step stabilization score was uncertain at the proposal stage, a four-step post-proposal investigation plan was specified before examining held-out data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Discrimination.} Measure how trajectory features separate safe from unsafe handovers and correlate with guidance strength across acoustic conditions.
\item \textbf{Controls.} Compare multi-signal policies against calibrated SNR-only gates, stage-wise SNR cascades of matched degrees of freedom, and cost-matched random step assignments.
\item \textbf{Conditions the SNR cannot see.} Evaluate zero-shot generalization on prompts degraded by reverberation, multi-talker babble, and telephone bandpass filtering.
\item \textbf{Richer zero-cost signals.} Explore trajectory statistics computed strictly from intermediate velocity tensors already materialized in GPU memory, without extra forward passes.
\end{enumerate}
Executing Steps~1--4 across subsequent Colab Tesla T4 development cohorts yielded three consolidated negative or exploratory findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{State-altering handover operators (\texttt{EXP-013}--\texttt{EXP-015}):} Eight candidate operators (endpoint extrapolation, coherence-gated extrapolation, trajectory consistency restart, spherical endpoint averaging, self-noise negative guidance, antithetic student sampling, SNR-controlled guidance, and post-handover error cancellation) failed preregistered routing criterion C3.
\item \textbf{Timing- and distribution-based handover controls (\texttt{EXP-030}, \texttt{EXP-031}):} Timing-only handover (\texttt{TOH3}, 12 passes) avoided copied white noise on the Tesla T4 ($+0.885$~UTMOS vs.\ \texttt{W4} pooled over \texttt{gauss25} and \texttt{noisy15} in \texttt{EXP-030}), but in \texttt{EXP-031} ($n=180$ across clean, white noise, reverberation, and DEMAND cafeteria babble; $20\times 12$ aligned pitch variety), both student-only (\texttt{STU}, 4 passes) and random per-seed re-timing (\texttt{RND}, 4 passes) matched \texttt{TOH3} on raw $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ and clean UTMOS (\texttt{teacher\_not\_shown\_needed}), while Chapter~3's policy (\texttt{CH3} $\lambda=0.35$ at $18.28$ passes; \texttt{CH3\_LTT} $\lambda=1.20$ at $16.33$ passes) still scored $1.250$ \texttt{noisy15} UTMOS vs.\ $1.970$ for \texttt{STU}. Post hoc, after sequence alignment, per-band quantile transfer (\texttt{QH3}) retained $64\%$ of \texttt{W4}'s aligned pitch-variety gain without copied white noise.
\item \textbf{Coarse-grid commit-point teacher path (\texttt{EXP-033}):} On the Tesla T4 ($n=60$ quality, $20\times 12$ clean variety, $10$ timed cases), walking to $t_8=0.2929$ in half the teacher steps and restarting the student at $j=2$ (\texttt{CPH8}, 12 passes, $1.842$~s) retained $94\%$ ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$) and $97\%$ (aligned) of \texttt{P25}'s clean variety gain at $-0.022$ UTMOS (\texttt{P25}: $30$ passes, $4.589$~s), though not confirmed at the 90\% bar with $n=20$ speakers; on \texttt{noisy15} prompts, all coarse teacher paths produced $7$--$11$ of $30$ runaway outputs unless gated to \texttt{STU} ($8.0$ mean passes post hoc vs.\ $19.2$ for Chapter~3's policy at $\lambda=0.35$).
\end{itemize}
Two theoretical principles emerged from synthesizing these exploratory probes (\nolinkurl{docs/progress/log/2026-11-15-handover-invariance-theory.md} and \nolinkurl{docs/progress/log/2026-11-17-spread-weighted-snr.md}). First, under the \emph{spread principle} (\texttt{EVAL-027}), broadband flat background floors (white noise, pink noise, and high-pass hiss) carry high spectral entropy that the guided teacher reproduces while the reverse-KL student drops them via mode shrinkage, whereas narrowband distractors or speech-shaped noises are handled similarly by both models; weighting the 10th-percentile background spectrum $N(f)$ by its spectral flatness $\mathrm{SFM}_N$ yields the spread-weighted SNR $\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{sw}} = 10\log_{10}(P_{\text{speech}}/\mathrm{mean}_f N(f)) - 10\log_{10}\mathrm{SFM}_N$. Second, the \emph{condition-invariance ladder} explains why Voice-Swap (\texttt{EXP-020}, shift-invariant), \texttt{TOH} (\texttt{EXP-030}, groupwise affine-invariant timing map), and \texttt{QH} (\texttt{EXP-031}, monotone-invariant quantile transfer) eliminate progressive levels of teacher condition leakage while trading amplitude precision for safety.

All \texttt{EXP-030}--\texttt{EXP-033} findings remain unapproved Colab T4 development evidence pending fresh-speaker confirmation, listening checks, literature audits, and adviser review; Chapter~\ref{sec:method} is unchanged. Appendix~\ref{app:snr-r} formalizes the invariance ladder and evaluates the follow-up two-signal invariant residual policy (\texttt{SNR\_R}, \texttt{EXP-034}) designed directly from these findings at the \texttt{CPH8} commit point. If the stabilization score adds no measurable value in subsequent held-out tests, the SNR-gated variant of the policy will be recommended and the stabilization score reported as a tested negative result; the contribution remains a per-utterance handover framework with a certified bound on added quality failures.

\subsection{Conceptual Framework and Mathematical Theory}
\label{app:snr-r-theory}

Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-dev} and \texttt{EXP-033} established why Chapter~\ref{sec:method}'s two signals struggle together when intermediate noise enters: the frame-energy estimator $\hat S$ (\Cref{eq:snr}, $\theta=20$~dB) rates $25$~dB white, pink, and high-pass noise above $20$~dB and lets it pass, while teacher drift $d_i$ (\Cref{eq:stab}) equals Euler's local truncation error up to step-size normalization, falls under acoustic noise (\emph{premature stabilization}), and cannot distinguish condition leakage from trajectory smoothness. At the same time, \texttt{EXP-033} and the transmissibility analysis of Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-variety} showed that four coarse Euler steps on an 8-interval sway grid ($10$ teacher evaluations to $t_4=0.2929$), restarted with $j=2$ ($\tau\in\{0.2929,0.6173\}$, $12$ passes total, \texttt{CPH8}), preserve clean pitch diversity while failing on degraded prompts unless replaced by the per-band quantile projection \texttt{CPQH}.

\phead{Condition model and invariance ladder} To separate teacher prosody from prompt corruption conceptually, consider log-mel estimates over $n_{\mathrm{gen}}$ generated frames and $B=100$ mel bands (\nolinkurl{docs/progress/log/2026-11-15-handover-invariance-theory.md}). Let $\hat x_1 \in \R^{n_{\mathrm{gen}}\times B}$ denote the teacher's commit-point estimate and $s_0 \in \R^{n_{\mathrm{gen}}\times B}$ the one-pass student draft from the same seed. A stationary recording condition with linear band gain $h_b>0$ and stationary additive noise floor $\nu_b\ge 0$ maps amplitude-domain log-mel frames $x$ monotonically in expectation via
\[
g_b(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\big(h_b\,e^{2x} + \nu_b\big), \qquad b=1,\dots,B.
\]
Viewing prompt conditions as transformations acting independently per band yields a nested hierarchy of condition groups $G_1 \subset G_2 \subset G_3$: per-band constant shifts $G_1$ ($x\mapsto x+c_b$), per-band positive affine maps $G_2$ ($x\mapsto a_b x+c_b$, $a_b>0$), and all per-band time-invariant strictly increasing maps $G_3$ (containing any stationary gain paired with a stationary noise floor). Under this decomposition, acoustic condition shifts per-band amplitude distributions, whereas rhythmic timing and relative pitch contour reside in each band's temporal ordering. Climbing the ladder (Table~\ref{tab:snr-r-ladder}) eliminates broader stationary condition classes while discarding progressively finer amplitude information from $\hat x_1$.

\phead{Formal properties of the quantile payload $Q$ and invariant residual $r$} Define the quantile handover payload per band $b$ as $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)_b(t) = \mathrm{sort}(s_{0,b})[\mathrm{rank}(\hat x_{1,b})(t)]$ over generated frames (with $s_0$'s prompt frames retained), and replace the previous-step teacher estimate in \Cref{eq:stab} by $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)$ to define the normalized invariant residual:
\[
r(\hat x_1, s_0) = \frac{\|\hat x_1 - Q(\hat x_1, s_0)\|_M}{\|\hat x_1\|_M + \epsilon},
\]
where $\|\cdot\|_M$ denotes the Euclidean/Frobenius norm on generated frames $M$ (\nolinkurl{code/colab/run_invariant_residual.py}, $\epsilon=10^{-12}$). Three mathematical properties (verified numerically in \nolinkurl{code/tests/test_invariant_residual.py}) govern $Q$ and $r$:

\begin{proposition}[$G_3$-invariance and maximal informativeness of $Q$]\label{prop:g3-qh}
Let entries within each generated band be distinct. A handover payload $H(\hat x_1, s_0)$ is $G_3$-safe ($H(g(\hat x_1), s_0) = H(\hat x_1, s_0)$ for every $g\in G_3$) if and only if it depends on $\hat x_1$ only through its per-band rank vectors. Consequently, $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)$ is $G_3$-invariant, maximally informative among $G_3$-safe payloads, draws every value exclusively from $s_0$'s spectral support, and satisfies $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)=s_0$ whenever $\hat x_{1,b}=\varphi_b(s_{0,b})$ for strictly increasing $\varphi_b$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because every $g_b\in G_3$ is strictly increasing, $x_t < x_{t'} \iff g_b(x_t) < g_b(x_{t'})$, so $\mathrm{rank}(g_b(\hat x_{1,b}))=\mathrm{rank}(\hat x_{1,b})$ and hence $Q(g(\hat x_1),s_0)=Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$. Conversely, two vectors with identical ranks share a $G_3$-orbit via strictly increasing piecewise-linear interpolation through sorted coordinate pairs, so any $G_3$-invariant map is constant on rank equivalence classes and functions solely through the rank vectors. Because distinct entries in $s_{0,b}$ ensure distinct rank permutations produce distinct output tensors, $Q$ retains the complete rank invariant without loss. When $\hat x_{1,b}=\varphi_b(s_{0,b})$ with $\varphi_b$ increasing, $\mathrm{rank}(\hat x_{1,b})=\mathrm{rank}(s_{0,b})$, so indexing $\mathrm{sort}(s_{0,b})$ restores $s_{0,b}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Time-rearrangement nullity of $r$]\label{prop:r-null}
The invariant residual satisfies $r(\hat x_1, s_0) = 0$ if and only if, in every mel band $b$, $\hat x_{1,b}$ and $s_{0,b}$ have identical empirical value multisets (i.e.\ $\hat x_{1,b}$ is a temporal permutation of $s_{0,b}$). Thus pure frame reordering or timing shifts never trigger $r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\|\hat x_1\|_M+\epsilon>0$, $r=0 \iff \hat x_1 = Q(\hat x_1, s_0)$ on mask $M$. By construction, $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)_b$ permutes $\mathrm{sort}(s_{0,b})$, so equality requires $\mathrm{sort}(\hat x_{1,b})=\mathrm{sort}(s_{0,b})$. Conversely, whenever both multisets coincide, assigning the $k$-th sorted value of $s_{0,b}$ to the frame holding the $k$-th sorted value of $\hat x_{1,b}$ recovers $\hat x_{1,b}$ identically, giving $\hat x_1 - Q(\hat x_1, s_0) = 0$ and $r=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact Wasserstein-2 energy and stationary-floor detection]\label{prop:r-w2}
Over $n_{\mathrm{gen}}$ generated frames across $B$ mel bands, the unnormalized squared residual equals the summed per-band 1D 2-Wasserstein energy between the teacher's and student draft's spectral value distributions:
\[
\|\hat x_1 - Q(\hat x_1, s_0)\|_M^2 = \sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{gen}}} \bigl(\hat x_{1,b,(k)} - s_{0,b,(k)}\bigr)^2 = n_{\mathrm{gen}}\sum_{b=1}^{B} W_2^2\!\left(\hat\mu_{\hat x_1, b},\,\hat\mu_{s_0, b}\right),
\]
where $(k)$ denotes ascending order statistics per band. Moreover, under any stationary corruption $g\in G_3$ of the teacher estimate, Proposition~\ref{prop:g3-qh} holds $Q(g(\hat x_1), s_0) = Q(\hat x_1, s_0)$ fixed while altering only $g(\hat x_1)$ in the numerator $\|g(\hat x_1) - Q(\hat x_1, s_0)\|_M$; in particular, starting from the clean matched range ($\hat x_{1,b,(k)}\ge s_{0,b,(k)}$), adding a positive stationary floor $\nu_b>0$ strictly increases $\|g(\hat x_1)-Q\|_M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In each band $b$, $Q(\hat x_1, s_0)_b$ pairs every order statistic $\hat x_{1,b,(k)}$ with $s_{0,b,(k)}$ at the exact frame holding $\hat x_{1,b,(k)}$. Summing squared coordinatewise residuals over all frames yields $\sum_k (\hat x_{1,b,(k)} - s_{0,b,(k)})^2$, which equals $n_{\mathrm{gen}} W_2^2(\hat\mu_{\hat x_1,b}, \hat\mu_{s_0,b})$ for 1D empirical distributions. Under $\hat x_1\mapsto g(\hat x_1)$ with $g\in G_3$, $Q$ remains invariant by Proposition~\ref{prop:g3-qh}, while $g_b(\hat x_{1,b}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\log(e^{2\hat x_{1,b}}+\nu_b) > \hat x_{1,b}$ shifts every coordinate strictly upward; whenever $\hat x_{1,b,(k)} \ge s_{0,b,(k)}$, every summand $(g_b(\hat x_{1,b,(k)}) - s_{0,b,(k)})^2$ grows strictly with $\nu_b>0$, increasing $\|g(\hat x_1)-Q\|_M$ (and raising normalized $r$ monotonically across physical noise floors $\nu_b\in\{10^{-6},10^{-5},10^{-4}\}$ as verified in \nolinkurl{code/tests/test_invariant_residual.py}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Theoretical scope boundaries: broadband spread vs.\ non-$G_3$ corruptions]\label{rem:snr-r-scope}
Two model limits constrain where Propositions~\ref{prop:g3-qh}--\ref{prop:r-w2} hold on real waveforms. First, genuine background noise carries frame-to-frame random texture rather than a purely deterministic map $g\in G_3$; consistent with the spread principle (Appendix~\ref{app:drift-plan}), high-entropy broadband floors (white noise, pink noise, high-pass hiss) are copied by the guided teacher but dropped by the reverse-KL student's mode shrinkage, creating large spectral divergence $W_2(\hat\mu_{\hat x_1,b},\hat\mu_{s_0,b})$ that drives $r$ upward. Second, non-stationary or temporal-convolutional distortions such as heavy reverberation (\texttt{reverb09}), narrow telephone bandpass filtering (\texttt{telephone}), and multi-talker babble lie outside $G_3$ and contaminate both $\hat x_1$ and the student draft $s_0$ alike, keeping their marginal value distributions closer and bounding what $Q$ and $r$ alone can separate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Protocol, Pre-Run Checks, and Preregistered Verdict}

Post-hoc inspection (\nolinkurl{code/analysis/exp034_diagnostics.py}) highlights three design trade-offs alongside the frozen verdict:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{\texttt{SNR\_R} vs.\ \texttt{R\_ONLY} and unconditional \texttt{CPQH}.} \texttt{SNR\_R} and \texttt{R\_ONLY} perform nearly identically overall ($\Delta\mathrm{UTMOS}=+0.0008$ $[-0.0071,+0.0089]$, $\Delta\mathrm{SIM}=+0.0004$), with $\hat S<20$~dB mainly adding conservative routing on \texttt{babble15}. Unconditional \texttt{CPQH} costs the same $13$ passes ($2.115$~s) with marginally higher overall SIM ($+0.0016$ $[+0.0004,+0.0028]$) and tie UTMOS ($-0.013$ $[-0.028,+0.002]$ vs.\ \texttt{SNR\_R}), so \texttt{SNR\_R}'s advantage over always running \texttt{CPQH} lies specifically in retaining $96\%$ vs.\ $87\%$ of \texttt{CPH8}'s clean aligned pitch variety.
\item \textbf{Teacher-free \texttt{STUR2} vs.\ all teacher paths.} Restarting the one-pass student draft at $j=2$ without any teacher pass (\texttt{STUR2}, $3$ passes, $0.511$~s, $0$ \texttt{noisy15} runaways) achieved the highest overall UTMOS ($3.114$), lowest WER ($0.0310$), and highest SIM ($0.8838$), beating even \texttt{CPQH} by $+0.151$ $[+0.104,+0.195]$ UTMOS and $-0.0166$ $[-0.0310,-0.0033]$ WER. However, \texttt{STUR2} loses clean prosodic diversity ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=0.0482$, aligned $0.0390$, $3.28$-frame spread). Adaptive teacher handover (\texttt{SNR\_R}) is therefore warranted only where multi-seed prosodic variety is required alongside robustness.
\item \textbf{Remaining blind spots and non-adoption status.} All methods degrade sharply under severe reverberation (\texttt{reverb09}, UTMOS $\approx 1.40$--$1.44$) and bandpass \texttt{telephone} filtering (UTMOS $\approx 1.49$--$1.61$), where both teacher and student absorb prompt channel distortion so $r$ cannot separate them. Because \texttt{EXP-034} uses $30$ development speakers (\texttt{positions 31--60}) rather than held-out evaluation speakers and applies fixed CPU-screen thresholds without held-out Learn-Then-Test risk certification, these results remain development evidence pending independent held-out evaluation, human listening checks, and team/adviser approval before modifying Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.
\end{enumerate}
