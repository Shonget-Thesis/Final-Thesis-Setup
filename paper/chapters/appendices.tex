\section[APPENDIX A.\ SAMPLE CALCULATIONS]{Sample Calculations}
\label{app:calc}

This appendix works through the formulas of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} with numbers small enough to check by hand. Sections~\ref{app:calc-grid} to \ref{app:calc-fail} follow one toy utterance through the stopping policy; Section~\ref{app:calc-ltt} computes the calibration p-values and the sample-size limit. In the toy example each ``output'' has only three numbers, the threshold is $\lambda=0.50$ and the SNR gate is $\theta=20$~dB. The values illustrate the computation and are not experimental results.

\label{app:calc-grid}

With \Cref{eq:cost}, stopping at $k=4$ costs $2(4+1)+3 = 13$ forward passes. Table~\ref{tab:app-grid} lists all grid points up to the reference.

\subsection{Prompt SNR Estimate}

Suppose the prompt has ten frames with energies 0.010, 0.012, 0.00001, 0.0002, 0.011, 0.009, 0.0003, 0.013, 0.010 and 0.0002. The quietest 10\% is one frame, so $\bar E_{\text{bottom}} = 0.00001$. The loudest half is 0.010, 0.010, 0.011, 0.012 and 0.013, so $\bar E_{\text{top}} = 0.0112$. By \Cref{eq:snr},
\[
\hat S = 10\log_{10}\frac{0.0112}{0.00001} = 10\log_{10}1120 = 30.5\ \text{dB}.
\]
Since $30.5\ge\theta=20$, the SNR gate is open and early stopping is allowed (Algorithm~\ref{alg:handover}, line~1).

\subsection{Endpoint Estimate}

At step 5 ($t_5=0.1181$), take one entry of the state, $x_{t_5}=0.8$, with guided velocity $v=1.5$ (\Cref{eq:guided}). By \Cref{eq:endpoint} the endpoint estimate of that entry is
\[
\hat x_1(t_5) = 0.8 + (1-0.1181)\times1.5 = 0.8 + 0.8819\times1.5 = 2.12.
\]
The velocity is computed anyway for the Euler step, so this estimate costs no forward pass (Proposition~\ref{prop:zero}).

\subsection{Endpoint-Stabilization Score and Stopping Decision}

Let the three-number endpoint estimates at steps 3, 4 and 5 be $[0.5,\,0.2,\,0.9]$, $[2.0,\,1.0,\,2.0]$ and $[2.2,\,1.0,\,1.6]$. With \Cref{eq:stab}:
\begin{itemize}
\item Step 4 (first candidate): the change is $[1.5,\,0.8,\,1.1]$ with norm $\sqrt{2.25+0.64+1.21}=\sqrt{4.10}=2.025$; the estimate's norm is $\sqrt{4+1+4}=3.000$; so $d_4 = 2.025/3.000 = 0.675 > \lambda = 0.50$. By \Cref{eq:policy} the teacher continues (Algorithm~\ref{alg:handover}, line~7).
\item Step 5: the change is $[0.2,\,0,\,-0.4]$ with norm $\sqrt{0.20}=0.447$; the estimate's norm is $\sqrt{4.84+1+2.56}=\sqrt{8.40}=2.898$; so $d_5 = 0.447/2.898 = 0.154 \le 0.50$, and $\hat S\ge\theta$. The teacher stops at step 5 (lines~4--5).
\end{itemize}
By \Cref{eq:cost} the utterance costs $2(5+1)+3 = 15$ forward passes instead of 21, a saving of $6/21 = 28.6\%$. Had $\hat S$ been 17.8~dB, the gate would have kept the teacher running to the reference step however settled the estimates looked, and the cost would have been 21.

\subsection{Added-Failure Check}
\label{app:calc-fail}

With \Cref{eq:failure} and $c=12.5\%$, $m_W=1$ point, $m_S=0.02$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No failure.} Reference output: WER 0\%, SIM 0.80. Candidate output: WER 0\%, SIM 0.79. The WER limit is $\max(12.5\%,\,0\%+1\%)=12.5\%$ and the SIM limit is $0.80-0.02=0.78$; both are respected, so $f=0$.
\item \emph{Failure.} Reference output: WER 15.38\% (2 errors in 13 words). Candidate output: WER 23.08\% (3 errors). The WER limit is $\max(12.5\%,\,15.38\%+1\%)=16.38\%$, which is exceeded, so $f=1$ regardless of SIM.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibration p-Values and Sample Size}
\label{app:calc-ltt}

Take $n=100$ calibration inputs, $\alpha=0.05$ and $\delta=0.10$, and suppose the thresholds of the grid produce the failure counts in Table~\ref{tab:app-ltt}. For brevity the example uses a four-value grid; the grid of the study is given in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}. Each p-value follows from \Cref{eq:pvalue}; for example, with one failure,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p &= \P\big(\mathrm{Bin}(100,0.05)\le1\big) = 0.95^{100} + 100\times0.05\times0.95^{99}\\
  &= 0.0059 + 0.0312 = 0.0371.
\end{aligned}
\]
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} certifies $\lambda=0.40$ and $\lambda=0.50$, stops at $\lambda=0.60$, and returns $\hat\lambda=0.50$. By \Cref{eq:guarantee}, with probability at least 90\%, this threshold adds failures to at most 5\% of new inputs from the same population.

\centering\small
\caption{Fixed-sequence calibration with $n=100$, $\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$ (illustrative failure counts).}
\label{tab:app-ltt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cccccl@{}}
\toprule
Order $g$ & $\lambda_g$ & Failures $n\hat R_g$ & $p_g$ (\Cref{eq:pvalue}) & $p_g\le0.10$? & Action \\
\midrule
1 & 0.40 & 0 & 0.0059 & yes & certify, continue \\
2 & 0.50 & 1 & 0.0371 & yes & certify, continue \\
3 & 0.60 & 3 & 0.2578 & no & stop \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\emph{Smallest calibration set with zero failures.} With no failures, \Cref{eq:pvalue} gives $p=(1-\alpha)^n$. Certifying $\alpha=0.05$ at $\delta=0.10$ needs $0.95^n\le0.10$, i.e.\ $n\ge\ln0.10/\ln0.95 = 44.9$. Indeed $0.95^{44}=0.105$ fails and $0.95^{45}=0.099$ passes, so at least 45 independent inputs are needed. With 36 inputs, $p=0.95^{36}=0.158>0.10$, which is why the earlier cross-validation (Appendix~\ref{app:prelim}) could not certify this tolerance.

\section[APPENDIX B.\ SAMPLE SIMULATION]{Sample Simulation}
\label{app:sim}

This appendix checks, on simulated data, whether the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:obj3} keeps its promise $\P(R\le\alpha)\ge1-\delta$, and compares it with a naive rule that picks the most aggressive threshold whose observed failure rate is at most $\alpha$. No speech is synthesized; the data are random numbers with the structure of Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Setup}

Each simulated utterance has a prompt SNR, stabilization scores $d_4,d_5,d_6$ at the three candidate steps, and an added-failure indicator for each candidate. A quarter of the utterances are noisy, with $\hat S\sim\mathcal N(16,2^2)$~dB; the rest are clean, with $\hat S\sim\mathcal N(33,4^2)$~dB. An utterance difficulty $h\sim\mathrm{LogNormal}(0,0.45^2)$ drives the scores, which shrink at later steps:
\[
d_k = h\, e^{-0.35(k-4)}\, u_k,\qquad u_k\sim\mathrm{LogNormal}(-0.55,\,0.25^2),\qquad k\in\{4,5,6\}.
\]
A candidate fails with a probability that grows with its score and is higher on noisy prompts:
\[
\P\big(f(x,k)=1\big) = \sigma\big(-4.4 + 3.2\,d_k + 1.6\,\mathbb 1[\text{noisy}] - 0.45\,(k-4)\big),
\]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function; the reference never fails, as in Definition~\ref{def:failure}. The policy of \Cref{eq:policy} uses $\theta=20$~dB and the grid $\lambda\in\{0.20,0.25,\dots,1.20\}$, with costs from \Cref{eq:cost}. The true risk of every threshold is computed on 400{,}000 simulated utterances (Figure~\ref{fig:app-sim}). Calibration is then repeated 1{,}000 times for each calibration size $n$, with $\alpha=0.05$ and $\delta=0.10$, and the true risk of each returned threshold is recorded. The script, \texttt{sim/ltt\_sim.py}, accompanies this manuscript.

\subsection{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:app-sim} summarizes the 1{,}000 repetitions. Learn-Then-Test met its promise at every calibration size: the returned threshold had true risk at most 5\% in 92.0\% to 98.8\% of repetitions, above the required 90\%. The naive rule did not: because it picks the threshold that happens to look best on one sample, its threshold exceeded the tolerance in more than half of the repetitions, even though its average risk was close to 5\%.

The price of the guarantee is computation. With 50 calibration inputs, Learn-Then-Test was conservative, certified no threshold in about one repetition in ten, and used 18.2 forward passes on average; with 400 inputs it used 15.9, close to the 15.4 of the largest safe threshold. This is the trade-off that the calibration-size analysis of Objective~4 (Section~\ref{sec:obj4}) will measure on real speech.

The simulation assumes independent inputs from one distribution, exactly the condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}. It therefore shows that the procedure is implemented and behaves as the theory says, not that the condition holds for real speech; that is what the held-out and repeated-split checks of Section~\ref{sec:obj3} will test. On the post-proposal Tesla T4 development runs reported in Appendix~\ref{app:stac} (\texttt{EXP-018}), both Learn-Then-Test \parencite{angelopoulos2025ltt} and one-dimensional Conformal Risk Control (CRC) \parencite{angelopoulos2024crc} at $\alpha=0.05$ empirically maintained continuous and binary quality losses within the target budget without an external prompt SNR gate.

\section[APPENDIX C.\ PROTOTYPE]{Prototype}
\label{app:prototype}

The study's prototype is an inference method rather than a user-facing application: the released DMOSpeech~2 sampler with the stopping controller of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} added. Figure~\ref{fig:glance} shows what the prototype changes for a user. The same networks produce the speech; only the point where the teacher hands over to the student moves, separately for each input, and that point never moves later than in the baseline.

The prototype will be packaged as one module shared by all experiment scripts (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). It takes the prompt audio, the prompt text, the target text, the calibrated threshold $\hat\lambda$ and the SNR gate $\theta$, and returns the waveform together with the chosen stopping step, the number of forward passes used, the prompt SNR estimate and the trace of stabilization scores. Before any experiment the module will pass the three checks of Section~\ref{sec:setup}: identical outputs for repeated runs with the same seed, exact reproduction of the reference when the controller falls back, and forward-pass counts equal to \Cref{eq:cost}.

The three boxes at the bottom of Figure~\ref{fig:glance} correspond to what the study must establish about the handover point: how it is decided (Objectives~1 and~2), how safe the decision is (Objective~3, Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}), and that it never costs more than the baseline (Proposition~\ref{prop:fallback}).

\subsection{Live Online Early-Stopping Verification on the Tesla T4}
\label{app:proto-live}

Following the proposal draft, the prototype sampler was executed with live online early stopping on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (\nolinkurl{research/experiments/EXP-018-swsr-handover-prelim/results/live_swsr_t4.csv}) under the Stage-Wise Trajectory Action Cascade (STAC) controller detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:stac}. Across 30 utterances (10 development speakers under clean prompts, 20~dB white Gaussian noise, and 15~dB noisy prompts), terminating the teacher ODE loop online at the chosen checkpoint produced bitwise-identical endpoint tensors relative to full trajectory replay ($\max|\Delta\hat x_1| = 0.0$), with $0.12$~ms of scalar gate overhead and $12{,}027.0$~MB peak GPU memory (Table~\ref{tab:live-t4}). Clean speech exited near Step~4 ($13.20$ NFE, $-37.1\%$, $1.49\times$ end-to-end wall-clock speedup and $1.68\times$ teacher ODE speedup), moderate 20~dB noisy prompts exited near Step~6.4 ($17.80$ NFE, $-15.2\%$, $1.21\times$ speedup), and 15~dB degraded prompts automatically fell back to the full Step-8 reference in 10 of 10 cases with zero added quality loss.

\subsection{Fixed Switching Times}

This analysis used all 500 speakers of the three splits (Section~\ref{sec:data}), each under the clean and the 15~dB noisy prompt condition (1{,}000 instances), synthesized on the Tesla T4 GPU with the student restart fixed, evaluated with the models of Section~\ref{sec:measures}, and scored with the added-failure rule of Definition~\ref{def:failure} against the reference switching time $s=0.25$ (Step~8; \nolinkurl{code/analysis/problem_validation_t4.py}). With one switching time for every input, the failure rate rose as the switch moved earlier, and almost all failures came from noisy prompts (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-fail}); the same ordering held on each split separately. The cheapest switching time that did not fail varied between inputs (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-oracle}), and the 179 instances that needed the reference were all noisy. A per-input choice made in hindsight would therefore use 14.85 forward passes on average instead of 21 ($-29\%$), whereas no single earlier switching time is safe for every input.

\subsection{Student Restart Level and Paper Baselines}

Restarting the student one grid point later ($j=2$, two student steps, the main configuration reported for DMOSpeech~2; \cite{li2025dmospeech2}) saves one forward pass. On the initial 48-instance CPU check, its outputs departed from the $j=1$ reference more often than those of the released default $j=1$ did, at every switching time tested: 26 versus 11 of 48 at $s=0.07$, 13 versus 8 at $s=0.10$, 9 versus 8 at $s=0.15$, and 14 versus 0 at $s=0.25$. This finding was subsequently verified at scale on the Tesla T4 across $360$ benchmark utterances (\texttt{EXP-017}): the paper's $14+2$ configuration ($j=2$, $30$ NFE) incurred $140$ of $360$ ($38.9\%$) added failures against the 16-grid Step-8 reference ($34.17\%$ WER on 15~dB noisy prompts vs.\ $24.16\%$), and exact Algorithm~2 ($32$ NFE) incurred $172$ of $360$ ($47.8\%$) added failures ($41.01\%$ noisy WER), whereas the 32-interval $s=0.25$ reference ($j=1$, $33$ NFE) matched 16-grid Step~8 in continuous quality ($6.84\%$ vs.\ $6.79\%$ WER; $0.810$ vs.\ $0.810$ SIM) at $+57\%$ higher cost ($5.03$~s vs.\ $3.23$~s). Meanwhile, 32-step teacher-only F5-TTS \parencite{chen2024f5} required $64$ NFE ($9.65$~s) and suffered $97.36\%$ WER on 15~dB noisy prompts due to unregularized classifier-free guidance noise amplification. The restart level is therefore fixed at $j=1$ ($\tau=0.0761$, three student passes) as a controlled setting, and the teacher-to-student switching time is the sole handover variable varied in this study.

\subsection{Stabilization Policy with a Cross-Validated Threshold}
\label{app:prelim-cv}

Unlike the other experiments in this appendix, this one was re-run on the Tesla T4 GPU with the evaluation models of Section~\ref{sec:measures}, on the 100 development speakers under the clean and the noisy15 prompt conditions (200 instances; \nolinkurl{code/analysis/cv_stabilization_t4.py}). The policy of Definition~\ref{def:policy}, with an SNR gate of 20~dB, was evaluated by four-fold speaker-grouped cross-validation. In each fold the threshold $\lambda$ was chosen on 75 speakers (150 instances) as the largest value in $\{0.20,0.25,\dots,0.60\}$ with zero added failures and frozen before scoring the 25 held-out speakers. The folds selected $\lambda=0.35$, 0.35, 0.35 and 0.50. Out of fold, mean cost fell from 21 to 18.88 forward passes ($-10.1\%$) with one added failure in 200 instances, against 50 of 200 for a fixed $s=0.15$, and the SNR gate sent all 100 noisy prompts to the reference, so the saving came from the clean prompts. Tables~\ref{tab:cv-policies} and~\ref{tab:cv-quality} compare it on the same 200 instances with the methods of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} (Table~\ref{tab:methods}), measured and tested as planned there. Against every fixed early switching time the policy had far fewer added failures (exact McNemar $p<10^{-13}$ for each), and the upper end of its 95\% interval for the failure rate, 2.8\%, stayed below the 5\% tolerance. Against the reference it used significantly fewer forward passes (Wilcoxon signed-rank test on speaker-level values, Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-15}$), while its differences in WER, SIM and UTMOS were not significant (Holm-adjusted $p=1.00$ for each); a claim that its quality equals the reference's needs the two one-sided tests with margins fixed before the held-out test. On clean prompts it saved 20.2\% of the passes and chose Steps~4, 5 and~6 for 6, 28 and 52 inputs, so the choice adapted per input. The SNR-gated controls behaved similarly on these data: SNR-only, the SNR gate followed by Step~5 or Step~6, and a random assignment of the policy's own steps among the gated inputs (2{,}000 draws, mean 0.75 failures) all stayed within 0 to 3 failures, and none differed significantly from the policy (exact McNemar $p\ge0.50$). Stabilization alone, without the gate, found no threshold with zero training failures in any fold and kept every input at the reference, because noisy prompts stabilize early. With at most three failures per method, these data cannot separate the SNR-gated variants; the comparison on the test split (Section~\ref{sec:drift-plan}) is designed for that. The earlier CPU version of this experiment, on 12 speakers with lighter evaluation models, had selected the edge of the search range, $\lambda=0.60$, in every fold; on the T4 data the selected thresholds lie inside the range, and widening it to 1.20 leaves them unchanged. Two limitations remain. Selecting a threshold with zero training failures is not a risk certificate: with 75 training speakers and no failures, the smallest risk that can be certified at $\delta=0.10$ is about 3\% (Appendix~\ref{app:calc-ltt}); after zero observed failures the upper confidence limit shrinks only in proportion to $1/n$, about $3/n$ at 95\% confidence \parencite{hanley1983zero}. And the development split also served other design decisions, so these out-of-fold figures may be optimistic, because reusing data that guided earlier choices to select and then report performance is a known source of selection bias \parencite{cawley2010overfitting}. Objective~3 replaces this selection with Learn-Then-Test on speaker-disjoint data with one utterance per speaker.

\emph{Verdict, post-proposal audit, and binary noise floor.} Taken together, the 200-instance cross-validation supported per-input switching on clean versus 15~dB prompts (1 failure against 50 to 66 of 200, saving 10.1\% of passes overall and 27\% of clean synthesis time). However, subsequent Tesla T4 audits (\texttt{AUDIT-001}, \nolinkurl{research/audits/2026-10-25/FINDINGS.md}) and multi-condition rehearsals (\texttt{EXP-012} and \texttt{EXP-017}, $n=360$) uncovered two structural limits of this initial formulation. First, on the 500-speaker T4 benchmark, fixed-sequence Learn-Then-Test on the 200 calibration speakers certified every grid value up to $\hat\lambda=1.20$ (reducing Definition~\ref{def:policy} to SNR-only at $17.04$ NFE), whereas across the six noise levels of \texttt{EXP-012}/\texttt{EXP-017}, Definition~\ref{def:policy}'s low-drift inequality ($d_i\le\lambda$) certified \emph{no} threshold on the 16-interval grid ($0.0\%$ saving, $21.00$ NFE) and saved only $1.8\%$ on the 32-interval grid. Second, comparing the two equal-quality references directly (32-grid $s=0.25$ versus 16-grid Step~8, $0.000$ mean SIM difference) still flagged $81$ of $360$ ($22.5\%$) binary ``added failures'' solely from stochastic student seed variance on noisy prompts. This $22.5\%$ binary noise floor motivates evaluating handover policies via paired continuous differences and Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence \parencite{schuirmann1987} in Appendix~\ref{app:stac}.

\subsection{Development-Split Signal Diagnostic and Premature Stabilization}
\label{app:prelim-dev}

A first diagnostic of the two signals was run on the 100 development speakers with the evaluation models of this study (Section~\ref{sec:measures}), each under the clean and the 15~dB noisy condition. On those two extreme conditions alone, the estimated prompt SNR separated the groups completely: 23.3 to 50.0~dB for clean prompts and 16.8 to 18.2~dB for 15~dB noisy prompts, so a 20~dB gate sent every 15~dB noisy prompt to the reference. Blind estimation of SNR from statistics of the waveform is established practice \parencite{kim2008wada}; the estimator of Definition~\ref{def:snr} uses a ratio of frame energies instead. However, when tested across intermediate and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) acoustic corruptions on the Tesla T4 (\texttt{EXP-011} and \texttt{EXP-012}), both initial signal assumptions broke down:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Prompt SNR failure on moderate and OOD noise.} Prompts degraded with 18 to 22~dB white Gaussian noise produced frame-energy estimates of 19.0 to 24.8~dB, crossing the $\theta=20$~dB gate and causing $86$ of $360$ ($23.9\%$) added failures---$4.8\times$ above the $5\%$ tolerance. On OOD corruptions (\texttt{EXP-011}, $n=100$), bandpass telephone filtering silenced pause frames so that the estimator reported a mean SNR of $44.2$~dB (``ultra-clean'') and prematurely exited on degraded speech, while 20~dB babble measured $23.6$~dB and waveform SNR estimation added $1.85$~ms of overhead per utterance.
\item \textbf{Premature stabilization of single-step drift.} On clean prompts, single-step $d_4$ showed near-chance failure discrimination (ROC area $\approx 0.47$ from two to three failures). Across noise levels (\texttt{EXP-012}, $n=360$), acoustic noise weakened classifier-free guidance contrast (Spearman $\rho=0.89$ with the conditional--unconditional gap, whose median fell from $0.236$ on clean prompts to $0.116$ at 15~dB), causing median $d_4$ to drop from $0.61$ (clean) to $0.17$ (15~dB noisy). Degraded trajectories therefore \emph{stabilize prematurely}: stopping when $d_i\le\lambda$ hands noisy prompts over first rather than last.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Plan for Further Investigation and Exploratory Candidates}
\label{app:drift-plan}

Because the value of the single-step stabilization score was uncertain at the proposal stage, a four-step post-proposal investigation plan was specified before examining held-out data (summarized in Section~\ref{sec:drift-plan}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Discrimination.} Measure how trajectory features separate safe from unsafe handovers and correlate with guidance strength across acoustic conditions.
\item \textbf{Controls.} Compare multi-signal policies against calibrated SNR-only gates, stage-wise SNR cascades of matched degrees of freedom, and cost-matched random step assignments.
\item \textbf{Conditions the SNR cannot see.} Evaluate zero-shot generalization on prompts degraded by reverberation, multi-talker babble, and telephone bandpass filtering.
\item \textbf{Richer zero-cost signals.} Explore trajectory statistics computed strictly from intermediate velocity tensors already materialized in GPU memory, without extra forward passes.
\end{enumerate}
Executing Steps~1--4 on the development cohort (\texttt{EXP-011} through \texttt{EXP-018}) showed that eight state-altering handover and guidance operators (\texttt{EXP-013}--\texttt{015}: endpoint extrapolation, coherence-gated extrapolation, trajectory consistency restart, spherical endpoint averaging, self-noise negative guidance, antithetic student sampling, SNR-controlled guidance, and post-handover error cancellation) failed preregistered routing criterion C3, while binary single-threshold gates (calibrated SNR and Mahalanobis Trajectory Anomaly Score) collapsed to $1.5\%$ NFE saving when Step~1 ($7$ NFE) entered the candidate set. Inverting the drift direction and summing cumulative trajectory path length resolved both issues while eliminating external SNR estimation entirely, yielding the Stage-Wise Trajectory Action Cascade (STAC) presented in Appendix~\ref{app:stac}.

\section[APPENDIX E.\ STAGE-WISE TRAJECTORY ACTION CASCADE]{Stage-Wise Trajectory Action Cascade and Multi-Grid Benchmarks}
\label{app:stac}

This appendix documents the novel handover policy and empirical benchmarks resulting from the post-proposal investigation plan of Appendix~\ref{app:drift-plan} (\texttt{EXP-018}, \nolinkurl{research/experiments/EXP-018-swsr-handover-prelim/results/SWSR_HANDOVER_REPORT.md}). The method preserves the exact scope of Chapters~1--3: teacher weights, distilled student weights, duration prediction, input prompt conditioning, and student restart level ($\tau=0.0761$, $j=1$) remain completely frozen; only the per-utterance teacher-to-student handover step is adapted. All experiments below were executed on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU in Google Colab and are recorded as development evidence prior to frozen unseen-speaker validation.

\subsection{Method: Cumulative Trajectory Action and Conformal Risk Control}
\label{app:stac-method}

\phead{Cumulative trajectory action} Let $\hat x_1(t_j)=x_{t_j}+(1-t_j)v_{\text{cfg}}(x_{t_j},t_j)$ denote the teacher's projected mel endpoint at sway-grid step $j$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}), and let $d_j(x)$ denote the relative step-to-step endpoint change (\Cref{eq:stab}). Rather than testing whether $d_k(x)$ has fallen below a single threshold, the \emph{Stage-Wise Trajectory Action Cascade} (STAC) accumulates the discrete trajectory path length---or \emph{trajectory action}---traveled by the guided teacher from $t_1$ through $t_k$:
\[
A_k(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} d_j(x), \qquad k \in \{1,\dots,k_{\text{ref}}-1\}.
\]
Because $d_j(x)$ reuses velocity tensors already computed for the Euler update, maintaining the running scalar sum $A_k(x) = A_{k-1}(x) + d_k(x)$ requires zero extra neural function evaluations and adds $0.12$~ms of wall-clock overhead per utterance, while eliminating the $1.85$~ms external waveform SNR estimator (Figure~\ref{fig:stac}).

\input{figures/fig_stac}

\phead{Stage-wise stopping rule} Given candidate handover steps $\mathcal{K}=\{k_1<\dots<k_{M-1}\}$ preceding reference step $k_{\text{ref}}$ and an offline-calibrated threshold vector $\hat{\bm\lambda}=(\hat\lambda_{k_1},\dots,\hat\lambda_{k_{M-1}})$, STAC hands over to the student at the earliest candidate step whose cumulative action reaches its stage threshold:
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{STAC}}(x) = \min\bigl\{\, k \in \mathcal{K} : A_k(x) \ge \hat\lambda_k \,\bigr\} \cup \{k_{\text{ref}}\}.
\]
Inverting the inequality ($A_k \ge \hat\lambda_k$) exploits the premature-stabilization phenomenon identified in Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-dev}: clean prompts induce strong classifier-free guidance updates and rapidly accumulate trajectory action, whereas noisy or bandwidth-degraded prompts exhibit suppressed guidance contrast and remain below $\hat\lambda_k$ until later steps or $k_{\text{ref}}$. Summing across steps acts as a discrete path-length odometer that filters single-step Euler jitter, preventing the premature exits of memoryless rules ($d_k\ge\hat\lambda_k$). On Utterance~1 of \texttt{EXP-018} ($\hat\lambda_4=1.59$, $\hat\lambda_7=1.93$ on \texttt{g16\_all8}), the clean prompt reached $A_4=3.65\ge 1.59$ and exited at Step~4 ($13$ NFE, $-38.1\%$); the 20~dB Gaussian prompt measured $A_4=1.52<1.59$, stayed in the teacher loop until $A_7=2.06\ge 1.93$, and exited safely at Step~7 ($19$ NFE, $-9.5\%$); and the 15~dB noisy prompt remained at $A_7=1.76<1.93$, completing all eight teacher steps ($21$ NFE) automatically.

\phead{Calibration: Stage-Wise empirical risk and 1D-CRC-STAC} Two offline calibration procedures are evaluated on speaker-disjoint folds:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Stage-Wise empirical cascade (\texttt{STAC}).} Proceeding sequentially across $k\in\mathcal{K}$ on calibration speakers, $\hat\lambda_k$ is chosen as the smallest action threshold whose routed calibration subset satisfies paired WER degradation $\Delta\overline{\mathrm{WER}}\le 0.01$ and speaker-similarity drop $\Delta\overline{\mathrm{SIM}}\ge -0.02$ relative to $k_{\text{ref}}$, allocating the total tolerance across active stages.
\item \textbf{One-Dimensional Conformal Risk-Controlled STAC (\texttt{1D-CRC-STAC}).} To attach a finite-sample distribution-free guarantee on continuous bounded loss without multi-dimensional grid inflation, stage thresholds $\lambda_k(\tau)$ are indexed by a single monotone aggressiveness parameter $\tau\in[0,1]$ over empirical calibration quantiles, so that bounded per-utterance quality loss $L_i(\tau)\in[0,1]$ is non-decreasing in $\tau$. Following \textcite{angelopoulos2024crc}, the calibrated parameter is
\[
\hat\tau = \sup\Bigl\{\, \tau\in[0,1] : \frac{n}{n+1}\hat R_n(\tau) + \frac{1}{n+1} \le \alpha \,\Bigr\}, \qquad \alpha = 0.05,
\]
guaranteeing $\E[L_{n+1}(\hat\tau)] \le \alpha$ for exchangeable unseen speakers while preserving per-stage threshold granularity.
\end{enumerate}
