\section[APPENDIX A.\ SAMPLE CALCULATIONS]{Sample Calculations}
\label{app:calc}

This appendix works through the equations and calibration procedure of Chapter~\ref{sec:method} on recorded values from the team's runs on Google Colab Tesla T4 GPUs (\nolinkurl{research/experiments/EXP-034-invariant-residual/results/quality.csv} and the \texttt{EXP-011} pilot \nolinkurl{worked_example.json}), using development-split speakers only. Sections~\ref{app:calc-grid} to~\ref{app:calc-fail} trace the candidate teacher paths, prompt SNR check, quantile projection, invariant residual, routing decision, and quality-failure rule on development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} (target text ``Some of the grey ash was falling off the circular edge.'', $11$ normalized words, $339$ prompt mel frames and $572$ total frames). Section~\ref{app:calc-ltt} then works through the three-path Learn-Then-Test selection of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} and the minimum calibration sample size of \Cref{eq:zerofail}.

\subsection{Candidate Teacher Paths and Forward-Pass Cost}
\label{app:calc-grid}

On the 8-interval sway grid ($N=8$ in \Cref{eq:grid}), grid point $i$ sits at $t_i = 1-\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\cdot\tfrac{i}{8})$. For the four-step candidate path ($k=4$ in \Cref{eq:paths}),
\[
t_4 = 1-\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{8}\Big) = 1-\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big) = 1-0.7071 = 0.2929,
\]
which equals $t_8^{(16)}$ of the released 16-interval grid and coincides with student schedule point $\tau_2=0.2929$ ($j=k/2=2$). Similarly, $k=2$ gives $t_2=1-\cos(\pi/8)=0.0761=\tau_1$ ($j=1$) and $k=6$ gives $t_6=1-\cos(3\pi/8)=0.6173=\tau_3$ ($j=3$). By Proposition~\ref{prop:cost} (\Cref{eq:cost}), path $k$ makes $e=k+1$ guided teacher evaluations ($2k+2$ passes), one student draft pass ($u=1$), and $n_s=4-k/2$ student finishing passes:
\[
C(4) = 2(4+1) + 1 + (4-2) = 10 + 1 + 2 = 13\ \text{passes},
\;
\Delta C = 1-\frac{13}{21} = 38.1\%.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:app-grid} lists all 8-interval grid points up to $t_6$ alongside the released 16-interval baseline.

\subsection{Prompt SNR Estimate}

To illustrate \Cref{eq:snr} directly from raw frame energies, in the \texttt{EXP-011} Tesla T4 trace of development utterance \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_13900-common_voice_en_13901} ($459$ prompt frames of $25$~ms at $10$~ms hop; quietest $10\%=46$ frames, loudest $50\%=230$ frames), the clean prompt has $\bar E_{\text{bottom}} = 1.747\times 10^{-5}$ and $\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.043\times 10^{-2}$, giving $\hat S = 10\log_{10}(0.02043/1.747\times 10^{-5}) = 30.68$~dB ($\ge\theta=20$~dB, gate open), whereas adding $15$~dB white noise raises $\bar E_{\text{bottom}}$ to $3.563\times 10^{-4}$ ($\bar E_{\text{top}} = 2.076\times 10^{-2}$) and drops $\hat S$ to $17.65$~dB ($<20$~dB, gate closed). On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} in \texttt{EXP-034}, the same estimator gives $\hat S=29.12$~dB on the clean prompt, $\hat S=17.69$~dB under $15$~dB white noise (caught by the gate), and $\hat S=25.50$~dB under $25$~dB white noise (\texttt{gauss25}) or $25.45$~dB under $25$~dB high-pass hiss (\texttt{hp25})---both above $\theta=20$~dB, leaving moderate noise to the invariant residual.

\subsection{Endpoint Estimate, Quantile Projection, Residual, and Routing}

At the commit point $t_k$, the teacher forms $\hat x_1 = x_{t_k} + (1-t_k)\,v(x_{t_k},t_k)$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}) at no pass beyond the final guided velocity evaluation; for instance, a mel entry with state $x_{t_4}=-0.6905$ and guided velocity $v=-0.5198$ at $t_4=0.2929$ gives $\hat x_1 = -0.6905 + (1-0.2929)(-0.5198) = -1.0580$. Independently from noise, one student evaluation at $\tau=0$ produces the draft $s_0 = \varepsilon_0 + g(\varepsilon_0,0)$ (\Cref{eq:draft}).

To see how the per-band quantile projection (\Cref{eq:quantile}) and invariant residual (\Cref{eq:residual,eq:w2}) work numerically, consider four illustrative generated frames in one mel band where the clean draft has values $s_{0,b}=(-3.8,-1.2,-4.0,-2.1)$ (sorted values $s_{0,b,(1..4)}=(-4.0,-3.8,-2.1,-1.2)$) and a noise-contaminated teacher band has $\hat x_{1,b}=(-1.9,-0.8,-1.1,-2.2)$ (where quiet pause frames were lifted by copied hiss):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Teacher time ranks.} Sorting $\hat x_{1,b}$ from quietest to loudest orders frames as $(4,1,3,2)$, so the frame ranks are $\operatorname{rank}_b=(2,4,3,1)$.
\item \emph{Quantile projection (\Cref{eq:quantile}).} Placing $s_{0,b,(\operatorname{rank}_b(t))}$ at each frame $t$ yields $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)_b = (-3.8,-1.2,-2.1,-4.0)$: the teacher's loudest frame ($t=2$, rank $4$) keeps the loudest clean value ($-1.2$), while the quietest frame ($t=4$, rank $1$) is restored from $-2.2$ to $-4.0$.
\item \emph{Residual and Wasserstein identity (\Cref{eq:residual,eq:w2}).} Framewise squared differences sum to $(-1.9+3.8)^2+(-0.8+1.2)^2+(-1.1+2.1)^2+(-2.2+4.0)^2 = 3.61+0.16+1.00+3.24 = 8.01$, which equals the sorted order-statistic sum $(-2.2+4.0)^2+(-1.9+3.8)^2+(-1.1+2.1)^2+(-0.8+1.2)^2 = 8.01 = 4\,W_2^2(\hat\mu_b,\hat\nu_b)$. With $\|\hat x_{1,b}\|^2 = 10.30$, this single band has relative residual $\sqrt{8.01}/\sqrt{10.30}=0.882$.
\end{enumerate}

Over all $n=233$ generated frames and $B=100$ mel bands of \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} at $k=4$ on the Tesla T4 (\texttt{EXP-034}), the recorded invariant residual is $r=0.2532$ on the clean prompt, $r=0.6844$ on \texttt{hp25}, and $r=0.7267$ on \texttt{gauss25}. With an illustrative threshold $\lambda=0.35$ and $\theta=20$~dB in \Cref{eq:policy}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Clean prompt} ($\hat S=29.12\ge 20$~dB, $r=0.2532\le 0.35$): neither condition triggers, so $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=\hat x_1$ (teacher payload kept).
\item \emph{High-pass 25~dB hiss} ($\hat S=25.45\ge 20$~dB, $r=0.6844>0.35$): the SNR gate stays open, but $r>\lambda$ triggers $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ (corrected payload).
\item \emph{White 15~dB noise} ($\hat S=17.69<20$~dB, $r=0.6480>0.35$): both checks trigger $\pi_{4,0.35}(x)=Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quality-Failure Rule and Excess-Failure Check}
\label{app:calc-fail}

Across the $30$ clean development prompts of \texttt{EXP-034}, the 5th percentiles (\Cref{eq:floors}) of the reproduced 21-pass baseline (\texttt{W8}) are $s_{\min}=0.8482$ and $u_{\min}=3.4031$, alongside the WER ceiling $c=12.5\%$ (\Cref{eq:failure}). Applying Definition~\ref{def:failure} and the excess-failure indicator $e(x,k,\lambda)=F(x,y_{k,\lambda}(x))\,(1-F(x,y_{\text{ref}}(x)))$ (\Cref{eq:excess}) at $k=4$, $\lambda=0.35$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Clean case ($e=0$, both pass).} On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} (\texttt{clean}), the baseline scores $\mathrm{WER}=9.09\%\le 12.5\%$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.971\ge 0.8482$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.697\ge 3.4031$ ($F_{\text{ref}}=0$), and the selected teacher payload $\hat x_1$ scores $(9.09\%,0.956,3.847)$ ($F=0$), giving $e=0\times(1-0)=0$.
\item \emph{Excess failure prevented by routing ($e=1\to 0$).} On development speaker \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_19833804-common_voice_en_19833805} under $25$~dB pink noise (\texttt{pink25}, $\hat S=26.94$~dB, $r=0.4535$), the 21-pass baseline passes all three bars ($\mathrm{WER}=0\%$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.931$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.749$, so $F_{\text{ref}}=0$). Without projection, $\hat x_1$ drops to $\mathrm{UTMOS}=2.785<3.4031$ ($F=1$, which would count as an excess failure $e=1\times(1-0)=1$); because $r=0.4535>0.35$, the router switches to $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$, restoring $\mathrm{UTMOS}=3.914$, $\mathrm{SIM}=0.935$, $\mathrm{WER}=0\%$ ($F=0$, so $e=0$).
\item \emph{Damaged prompt where the baseline itself fails ($F_{\text{ref}}=1\implies e=0$).} On \nolinkurl{common_voice_en_203357-common_voice_en_203358} under \texttt{noisy15}, the 21-pass baseline copies white noise ($\mathrm{SIM}=0.670<0.8482$, $\mathrm{UTMOS}=1.253<3.4031$, so $F_{\text{ref}}=1$), illustrating why Definition~\ref{def:failure} does not assume a failure-free baseline; routing to $Q$ cuts WER from $90.9\%$ (unprojected $\hat x_1$) to $9.09\%$ and yields $e=F\times(1-1)=0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three-Path Calibration p-Values and Minimum Sample Size}
\label{app:calc-ltt}

In Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt}, $\delta=0.10$ is split equally across the $|\mathcal{K}|=3$ candidate paths, so each path tests its ordered threshold grid $\Lambda=\{0.00,0.05,\dots,1.00\}$ at level $\delta/3 = 0.0333$ using the exact binomial p-value of \Cref{eq:pvalue}. At the study's calibration size $n=200$ and $\alpha=0.05$,
\[
\P\big(\mathrm{Bin}(200,0.05)\le 4\big) = \sum_{j=0}^{4}\binom{200}{j}0.05^j\,0.95^{200-j} = 0.0287 \le 0.0333,
\]
whereas $5$ excess failures give $\P(\mathrm{Bin}(200,0.05)\le 5) = 0.0623 > 0.0333$ (matching Table~\ref{tab:samplesize}). Table~\ref{tab:app-ltt} illustrates how Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} steps through one path's ordered grid at $n=200$ and then selects $\hat k = \min\{k\in\mathcal K : \hat\lambda^{(k)}\ne\textsc{none}\}$.

\emph{Smallest calibration set with zero excess failures.} With $n\hat R_{k,g}=0$, \Cref{eq:pvalue} simplifies to $p_{k,g}=(1-\alpha)^n$. Under \Cref{eq:zerofail} with $|\mathcal{K}|=3$, $\alpha=0.05$, and $\delta=0.10$, accepting a zero-failure candidate requires
\[
0.95^n \le \frac{0.10}{3} = 0.0333
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n \ge \frac{\ln(0.10/3)}{\ln(0.95)} = \frac{-3.4012}{-0.05129} = 66.31.
\]
Indeed $0.95^{66}=0.0339>0.0333$ fails whereas $0.95^{67}=0.0322\le 0.0333$ passes, giving the $67$-speaker minimum of Section~\ref{sec:calsize} (and explaining why the $30$-speaker \texttt{EXP-034} pilot has $0.95^{30}=0.2146$ and cannot itself certify $\alpha=0.05$, whereas a single fixed path $|\mathcal{K}|=1$ requires $\lceil\ln(0.10)/\ln(0.95)\rceil=45$ speakers).

\section[APPENDIX B.\ SAMPLE SIMULATION]{Sample Simulation}
\label{app:sim}

This appendix verifies on synthetic populations that the three-path Learn-Then-Test selection of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} satisfies Proposition~\ref{prop:ltt}'s guarantee $\P(R(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)\le\alpha)\ge 1-\delta$, and contrasts it with a naive uncalibrated rule that selects the cheapest path and largest threshold whose sample excess-failure rate is at most $\alpha$. No speech is synthesized; all numbers are reproduced by \nolinkurl{code/analysis/ltt_sim.py}.

\subsection{Setup}

Each simulated utterance draws one of the eight prompt conditions with equal probability (clean; heavy low-SNR noise around $17.5$~dB; three moderate $25$~dB noises around $26.2$~dB that pass the $\theta=20$~dB SNR gate; and two non-stationary channel conditions around $28.5$~dB), together with a nonzero baseline failure indicator $F_{\text{ref}}\in\{0,1\}$ matching Definition~\ref{def:failure} ($5\%$ failure on clean prompts, $55\%$--$70\%$ on degraded prompts). Across the three candidate teacher paths $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$ ($10$, $13$, and $16$ passes; fallback $21$ passes), residuals $r^{(k)}$ concentrate near $0.24$ on clean inputs and $0.54$--$0.58$ on moderate and heavy noise. To stress-test both path selection and threshold selection simultaneously, the $10$-pass path ($k=2$) is constructed to be slightly unsafe at every threshold ($\min_\lambda R(2,\lambda)=0.0629>0.05$), whereas the $13$-pass path ($k=4$) is safe up to $\lambda=0.50$ ($R(4,0.50)=0.0383$, $R(4,0.55)>0.05$) and the $16$-pass path ($k=6$) is safe up to $\lambda=0.55$ ($R(6,0.50)=0.0283$; Figure~\ref{fig:app-sim}). Population risks $R(k,\lambda)$ are evaluated on $400{,}000$ draws, after which calibration is repeated $1{,}000$ times at each sample size $n\in\{50,75,100,200,400\}$ with $\alpha=0.05$ and $\delta=0.10$ ($\delta/3=0.0333$ per path).

\subsection{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:app-sim} summarizes the $1{,}000$ calibration repetitions per sample size:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Exact finite-sample validity and the $n\ge 67$ floor.} At $n=50<67$, Learn-Then-Test certifies no candidate ($100\%$ safe fallback to the 21-pass baseline), confirming \Cref{eq:zerofail}. Across $n\in\{75,100,200,400\}$, Learn-Then-Test satisfies $R(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)\le 0.05$ in $97.4\%$ to $99.8\%$ of repetitions---well above the $90\%$ union-bound guarantee---and rejects the unsafe $10$-pass path ($k=2$) in $\ge 98.9\%$ of repetitions. By contrast, the naive rule picks the unsafe $k=2$ path in $25.4\%$--$52.4\%$ of repetitions and violates $\alpha=0.05$ in $59.9\%$--$79.9\%$ of runs ($\P(R\le\alpha)=0.201$--$0.401$).
\item \textbf{Sample size vs.\ path selection.} Because both $n=75$ and $n=100$ permit zero excess failures at level $\delta/3=0.0333$ (Table~\ref{tab:samplesize}), both are conservative ($44.6\%$ and $59.0\%$ fallback). At the study's planned $n=200$ (up to $4$ excess failures allowed), fallback drops to $5.2\%$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} selects the optimal $13$-pass path ($\hat k=4$) in $65.6\%$ of repetitions and $k=6$ ($16$ passes) in $28.8\%$ (mean cost $14.27$ passes, median $\hat\lambda_r=0.40$), and by $n=400$ it selects $\hat k=4$ in $91.5\%$ of runs ($13.26$ passes).
\end{enumerate}

\section[APPENDIX C.\ PROTOTYPE]{Prototype}
\label{app:prototype}

The study's prototype is an inference-time module and local demonstration (\texttt{code/\allowbreak prototype/}) rather than an end-user clinical application: it wraps the released DMOSpeech~2 networks with the payload router of Section~\ref{sec:architecture} without altering any model weights. Figure~\ref{fig:glance} summarizes what the prototype changes relative to the released sampler. Every utterance runs the same shortened teacher path and student schedule, while the router decides per utterance \emph{what} crosses at the handover---passing the teacher's own endpoint estimate $\hat x_1$ when safe, or replacing its amplitude distribution with the clean student draft's via $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ when prompt noise or a large invariant residual $r$ is detected.

Because the development pilot of Appendix~\ref{app:snr-r} (\texttt{EXP-034}) evaluated the midpoint four-step path ($k=4$, $t_4=0.2929$, $j=2$, $13$ forward passes), the current demonstration (\texttt{code/\allowbreak prototype/\allowbreak snr\_invariant.py}, \texttt{engine.py}, \texttt{server.py}, and \texttt{cli.py}) executes $k=4$ on a central processing unit using development presets ($\lambda_r=0.342$ and $0.382$ from \texttt{EXP-034}, rather than the held-out $(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)$ that Algorithm~\ref{alg:ltt} will select on the calibration split). For any selected development speaker or uploaded prompt, the web interface streams the one-pass student draft $s_0$, the guided 8-interval teacher steps to $t_4$, the prompt SNR $\hat S$ against $\theta=20$~dB, the per-band invariant residual $r$, the selected payload (\texttt{CPH8} for $\hat x_1$ vs.\ \texttt{CPQH} for $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$), and optional side-by-side synthesis of the 21-pass released baseline from identical seeds. Table~\ref{tab:samples} lists three sample conditions on one development speaker.

\section[APPENDIX D.\ PRELIMINARY EXPERIMENTS]{Preliminary Experiments}
\label{app:prelim}

This appendix reports the Google Colab Tesla T4 development experiments that tested the earlier concept-defense stopping-time scheduler and motivated the payload-router architecture of Chapter~\ref{sec:method}. Every run used the evaluation models of Section~\ref{sec:measures} (\texttt{Whisper-large-v3}, \texttt{WavLM-Base+}, \texttt{UTMOS}, and \texttt{pYIN}); only Colab T4 GPU runs are included. Because calibration and test speakers entered the initial 500-speaker exploratory grid before the confirmatory protocol was fixed, all results here remain development evidence rather than held-out proof. Table~\ref{tab:prelim} summarizes each experiment and the specific Chapter~\ref{sec:method} design choice it informed.

The earlier concept-defense scheduler operated on the released 16-interval sway grid with $j=1$ ($3$ student passes) and attempted to vary \emph{when} the teacher stopped per utterance ($k\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}$, costing $13$--$21$ passes). It measured consecutive endpoint change with the endpoint-stabilization score
\begin{equation}
d_i = \frac{\|\hat x_1(t_i) - \hat x_1(t_{i-1})\|_M}{\|\hat x_1(t_i)\|_M + \epsilon},
\label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
\eqentry{Endpoint-stabilization score (earlier proposal)}
\begin{whereterms}[2.6cm]
$d_i$ & endpoint-stabilization score at 16-grid teacher step $i$; small means settled \\
$\hat x_1(t_i)$ & endpoint estimate at step $i$ (\Cref{eq:endpoint}) on the 16-interval grid \\
$\|\cdot\|_M$ & Euclidean norm over the generated frames \\
$\epsilon$ & small positive constant against division by zero
\end{whereterms}
and stopped at the earliest candidate step where $d_i\le\lambda$ and $\hat S\ge 20$~dB, falling back to Step~8 ($21$ passes) otherwise, judged under an earlier baseline-relative failure rule ($\mathrm{WER}>\max(12.5\%,\mathrm{WER}_{\text{ref}}+1\%)$ or $\mathrm{SIM}<\mathrm{SIM}_{\text{ref}}-0.02$, with the Step-8 baseline treated as failure-free).

\subsection{Prosodic Variety and Student Restart Transmissibility}
\label{app:prelim-variety}

Multi-seed prosodic variety experiments on the Tesla T4 (\texttt{EXP-020}, \texttt{EXP-022}, \texttt{EXP-026}--\texttt{EXP-032}; $12$--$20$ seeds per speaker) examined why DMOSpeech~2 uses a teacher at all and how student re-noising affects retained melody:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Variety loss under $j=1$ vs.\ $j=2$ (\texttt{EXP-032}, Table~\ref{tab:prelim-variety-steps}):} Under the released $j=1$ restart ($\tau_1=0.0761$, re-noising $92.4\%$ of the handover tensor), clean $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ plateaued by Step~2--3, and 16-grid Step~8 (\texttt{W8}, $21$ passes) kept only $60\%$ of the paper configuration's (\texttt{P25}, $30$ passes) raw $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ gain over student-only generation and only $35\%$ $[9\%,53\%]$ of its DTW-aligned pitch-variety gain. Vocoding intermediate mel tensors confirmed that by Step~8 the teacher already matched \texttt{P25}'s melody within one semitone on $82\%$ of voiced frames, but restarting at $j=1$ washed retention down to $53\%$ (vs.\ $79\%$--$86\%$ at $j=2$, $\tau_2=0.2929$).
\item \textbf{Restart transmissibility and trade-off (\texttt{EXP-026}--\texttt{EXP-029}, Table~\ref{tab:prelim-variety-r2}):} Modeling a teacher-committed prosodic mode $\pm m$ with posterior log-odds $\ell_k$ across student re-noising $\phi=\tau m\tanh(\ell_k/2)+(1-\tau)\varepsilon'$ yields mode-survival probability $R_k = \Phi\big((A|\tanh(\ell_k/2)|-\beta)/B\big)$ with $A=2m^2\tau^2/(1-\tau)^2$ and $B=2m\tau/(1-\tau)$. Raising $\tau$ from $0.0761$ ($j=1$) to $0.2929$ ($j=2$) multiplies $A/B$ by $\approx 5\times$, explaining why restarting at $\tau=0.2929$ (\texttt{W8R2}, $20$ passes) recovered pure-teacher pitch variety on $40$ fresh speakers ($+0.0121$ $[+0.0081,+0.0159]$ over \texttt{W8}, $p=8.36\times 10^{-6}$ in \texttt{EXP-027}), while earlier unaligned 16-grid $j=2$ stops (\texttt{W4R2} at $t_4^{(16)}=0.0761\ne 0.2929$) hurt clean UTMOS ($-0.373$) because the student restarted at a noise level mismatching where the teacher stopped. Matching the student's restart schedule $\tau_{k/2}$ to the teacher's commit point $t_k$ on the 8-interval grid directly defines Chapter~\ref{sec:method}'s candidate path set $\mathcal{K}=\{2,4,6\}$ (\Cref{eq:paths}) and motivates testing router $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ against \texttt{STUR2} (Section~\ref{sec:stats}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the Earlier Stopping-Time Scheduler Failed Under Multi-Noise Calibration}
\label{app:prelim-cv}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{SNR gate leakage on moderate and band-limited noise.} White noise at $18$--$22$~dB yielded $\hat S\in[19.0,24.8]$~dB, leaking past $\theta=20$~dB ($86/360 = 23.9\%$ failures), while telephone bandpass filtering silenced pause frames so $\hat S$ averaged $44.2$~dB (``ultra-clean'') and $20$~dB babble averaged $23.6$~dB.
\item \textbf{Premature stabilization of $d_i$.} By the Euler step and \Cref{eq:endpoint}, $\hat x_1(t_i)-\hat x_1(t_{i-1})=(1-t_i)(v_i-v_{i-1})$, so $d_i$ (\Cref{eq:stab}) is proportional to Euler's local truncation error rather than prompt contamination. Acoustic noise weakened classifier-free guidance contrast ($\rho=0.89$ with $\|v_c-v_u\|$), dropping median $d_4$ from $0.61$ (clean) to $0.17$ ($15$~dB noise). Stopping when $d_i\le\lambda$ therefore exited \emph{earliest} on noisy prompts, and 5-fold Learn-Then-Test ($\alpha=0.05$, $\delta=0.10$) certified \textbf{no} stopping threshold ($0.0\%$ saving, $21.00$ passes in both \texttt{EXP-017} and \texttt{EXP-018}, bottom rows of Table~\ref{tab:t4-steps1to8-policies}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Exploratory Handover Probes Leading to Payload Routing}
\label{app:drift-plan}

Follow-up T4 development probes tested how to save teacher passes without copying prompt noise:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{State-altering and band-swap operators (\texttt{EXP-013}--\texttt{EXP-015}, \texttt{EXP-020}, \texttt{EXP-022}):} Eight velocity/extrapolation operators failed routing criterion C3; per-band mean replacement (Voice-Swap, \texttt{EXP-020}) cut white-noise failures from $126$ to $74/300$ ($p=2.3\times 10^{-6}$) without losing clean variety, pointing toward per-band normalization ($G_1$ in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).
\item \textbf{Quantile transfer and coarse teacher paths (\texttt{EXP-030}, \texttt{EXP-031}, \texttt{EXP-033}):} Per-band quantile transfer (\texttt{QH3}, $G_3$ in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}) removed copied white noise while retaining $64\%$ of aligned variety gain in \texttt{EXP-031}. Meanwhile, stepping along the 8-interval grid to $t_4=0.2929$ with $j=2$ (\texttt{CPH8}, $12$ passes, $1.84$~s in \texttt{EXP-033}) retained $94\%$ ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$) and $97\%$ (aligned) of \texttt{P25}'s clean variety gain ($30$ passes, $4.59$~s), but still suffered $7/30$ \texttt{noisy15} runaways unless paired with a clean-payload route.
\end{itemize}
Synthesizing these findings produced the two principles formalized in Section~\ref{sec:obj2}: (1)~high-entropy broadband noise floors are copied by the guided teacher but dropped by the distilled student draft $s_0$, creating a measurable 1D Wasserstein shift $r(\hat x_1,s_0)$ (\Cref{eq:w2}) even when $\hat S\ge 20$~dB; and (2)~routing between $\hat x_1$ and $Q(\hat x_1,s_0)$ on a shortened 8-interval path ($k\in\{2,4,6\}$, selected offline with LTT) decouples computational cost from per-utterance noise protection.

Three diagnostic findings directly shaped the evaluation design of Section~\ref{sec:obj4}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Ablations on the selected path.} Because \texttt{R\_ONLY} and unconditional \texttt{CPQH} closely approached \texttt{SNR\_R} in mean quality ($2.949$ and $2.963$ vs.\ $2.950$ UTMOS, with \texttt{SNR\_R}'s main edge over \texttt{CPQH} lying in $96\%$ vs.\ $87\%$ aligned clean pitch variety), Table~\ref{tab:systems} tests always projecting, never projecting, and \texttt{SNR-only} explicitly on the selected path $\hat k$.
\item \textbf{Pre-specified variety test against \texttt{STUR2}.} Teacher-free \texttt{STUR2} ($3$ passes, $0.511$~s) achieved the highest overall UTMOS ($3.114$) and lowest WER ($0.0310$) but lost clean prosodic variety ($\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}=0.0482$, aligned $0.0390$). Section~\ref{sec:stats} therefore fixes the paired $\mathrm{CV}_{f_0}$ test of the router against \texttt{STUR2} as the primary test of whether keeping any teacher path is justified.
\item \textbf{Non-$G_3$ channel limits and pending confirmatory runs.} All evaluated arms degraded under heavy room echo (\texttt{reverb09}, $1.40$--$1.43$ UTMOS) and narrowband \texttt{telephone} filtering ($1.49$--$1.61$ UTMOS), where both $\hat x_1$ and $s_0$ absorb convolutional distortion (Section~\ref{sec:condition-model}). Moreover, \texttt{EXP-034} evaluated only $k=4$ on $30$ development speakers without multi-path Learn-Then-Test selection; evaluating $k\in\{2,4,6\}$, selecting $(\hat k,\hat\lambda_r)$ on the $200$ calibration speakers, and testing on the $200$ held-out Seed-TTS speakers and LibriSpeech-PC constitute the confirmatory stages of Chapter~\ref{sec:method}.
\end{enumerate}
