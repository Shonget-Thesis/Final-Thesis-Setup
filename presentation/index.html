Section 1.2 · Problem Statement

Limitations of Static Handover Schedules Across Inputs

Input Aclean, easy
Input Bmoderate
Input Cnoisy prompt

FIXED HANDOVER th = 0.293 · 21 PASSES EACH

Too late wastes guided passes. Too early may degrade speech. Does every utterance need 18 teacher passes?

Preliminary T4 evidence · Appendix D.1 · 1,000 instances

Fixed early switches fail on 25–33% of inputs (noisy prompts 49–64%, clean 1.0–2.4%). A per-input choice in hindsight needs 14.85 of 21 passes (−29%). No single early switch is safe for every input.

1.2.1

Uncharacterized effects. How the switching time changes WER, SIM, UTMOS and cost per utterance is unmeasured.

1.2.2

The handover boundary. Can inference-time signals pick an earlier switch without losing quality?

1.2.3

No reliability guarantee. Adaptive selection alone says nothing about the rate of added failures.

1.2.4

Application constraints. Faster synthesis is not evidence of benefit to autistic users.

SourcesLi et al. (2026), DMOSpeech 2, AAAI · Ho & Salimans (2022) · Angelopoulos et al. (2025), Learn then test, Ann. Appl. Stat. · Proposal Appendix D.1 (development evidence)

General & Specific Research Objectives

1

Characterize

Measure how WER, SIM, UTMOS, forward passes and runtime vary across candidate switching times and inputs.

2

Formulate

Pose selection as constrained cost minimization; design a training-free policy with no extra network evaluations; prove its properties.

→ policy + 4 propositions
3

Calibrate

Use Learn-Then-Test to keep added-failure risk within a tolerance α at confidence 1 − δ; check it on held-out speakers.

Sections 2.4–2.8 · Literature Synthesis

Where Prior Work Leaves the Gaps

1

Adaptive, but no bound on quality loss

Per-input rules built on a learned halting unit or a hand-tuned threshold. None states how much quality the saving may cost.

2

Bounded, but not for a handover

CALM and adaptive ASR correction calibrate a threshold with risk control, for exit layers and recognition hypotheses, not for a teacher-to-student switch in speech synthesis.

3

A handover, but one fixed for all inputs

DMOSpeech 2 switches at one time for every utterance; T-Stitch uses a preset switching point. Neither adapts to the input or bounds the risk.

★

The centre is unoccupied

No reviewed method pairs a per-utterance handover with a calibrated bound. This describes the sources examined, not every adaptive method that exists.

Prop. 3.2

Zero overhead

Signals reuse the velocity already computed. No extra forward pass.

Prop. 3.3

Monotone cost

A smaller λ never stops earlier, so cost rises as λ falls.

Prop. 3.4

Safe fallback

Never more than 21 passes. No certified λ → exact reference output.

Prop. 3.5

Oracle bound

No zero-risk policy beats the cheapest non-failing candidate per input.

Contribution & Summary

What This Study Adds

Within the sources reviewed, no method combines all three for flow-matching TTS.

Prior work
This study
01When to hand over

One switching time for every utterance: fixed at 0.25 in DMOSpeech 2, preset in T-Stitch.

Chosen per utterance, from signals the sampler already computes: the prompt’s SNR and the endpoint-stabilization score.

02How sure we can be

Adaptive rules use learned or hand-tuned thresholds with no bound on quality loss. Calibrated bounds exist, but for exit layers and ASR hypotheses.

A Learn-Then-Test threshold: added failures ≤ α = 0.05 with probability ≥ 90%, checked on held-out speakers.

03What it costs

Some need extra training, or a quality estimator run on a full output.

No training and no extra forward pass. With no certified threshold it falls back to the released sampler, so it never costs more.

Not claimedA proven advantage of the hybrid over its parts, any change to duration prediction, or any new model training.

Moreover, comparing two equal-quality references still flagged 81/360 (22.5%) binary “failures” from student seed noise, motivating paired TOST evaluation.

While θ = 20 dB separated clean from 15 dB white noise, it missed 18–22 dB noise (86/360 = 23.9% failures, 4.8× over 5% budget) and was fooled by bandpass telephone audio (44.2 dB) and babble (23.6 dB), while adding 1.85 ms waveform overhead.

EXP-012 Discovery · Noise causes premature stabilization

On clean prompts, single-step d4 had AUC ≈ 0.47 (2–3 failures). Across noise levels (n = 360), degraded prompts stabilize prematurely: median d4 drops from 0.61 (clean) to 0.17 (15 dB noisy) as teacher guidance contrast collapses (ρ = 0.89). Thus di ≤ λ stops noisy prompts first.

EXP-013–018 Resolution · Cumulative trajectory action

Eight state-altering operators (DEH, TCR, cDEH, SEA, SNNG, SCG, PIEC) failed routing criterion C3, and 1-cut SNR gates collapsed to 1.5% saving when Step 1 was included. Summing path length Ai = ∑j=1i dj ≥ λ̂i flips the direction, smooths step jitter, and removes Prompt SNR.
