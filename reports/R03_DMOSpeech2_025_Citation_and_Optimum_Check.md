# Task R03: All-in-One Handover Statement & Exact Citation Verification

## 1. The All-in-One Statement

> **"Originally, the F5-TTS Teacher (Chen et al., 2025) used a 32-step grid (64 passes under Classifier-Free Guidance; Ho & Salimans, 2022) and showed that cutting it in half to a 16-step sway grid gives the same speech quality, while DMOSpeech 1 (Li et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2024) compressed the Teacher into a fast 4-step Student trained on four discrete noise levels starting at Step 4 ($t_4 \approx 0.08$). In DMOSpeech 2 (Li et al., 2026), the paper's Section 4.1 described the handover on the 32-step grid at `0.25` (14 Teacher steps + 2 Student steps), but their released `infer.py` code uses the 16-step Teacher grid with 3 Student steps—where `0.25` stops at Step 8 ($t_8 \approx 0.29$, half of 16) and costs 21 passes: 16 passes for the 8 Teacher Euler steps, plus 2 extra Teacher passes at Step 8 to compute the final endpoint estimate handed to the Student (`16 + 2 = 18` Teacher passes; Lipman et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026), plus 3 Student passes (`18 + 3 = 21` passes total). Because DMOSpeech 2 only tested `0.25` (Step 8) in Table 1—even though their own code defaults to `0.07` (Step 4, 13 passes) and their Section 3.3.2 suggests `0.10–0.20` (Steps 5 and 6 at `0.10` and `0.15`, 15 and 17 passes; matching the 20%–40% early switching window of Pan et al., 2025) for efficiency—our thesis tests all four candidates $\{0.07, 0.10, 0.15, 0.25\}$: we exclude Steps 1–3 because the Teacher has not reached the Student's trained starting level $\tau_1 \approx 0.08$ at Step 4 yet (Yin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025), we skip Step 7 (19 passes) because it saves only 2 passes over the zero-failure Step 8 reference (21 passes), and we do not go past Step 8 because DMOSpeech 2 Table 1 (Li et al., 2026) and F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025) show Step 8 already beats the full Teacher and later Teacher work is washed out when the Student re-noises the handover state back to $\tau_1 \approx 0.08$ (Meng et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2024)."**
