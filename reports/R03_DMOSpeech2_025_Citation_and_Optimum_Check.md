## 1. The All-in-One Statements

### 1A. Grid, 21-Pass Accounting, and Candidate Set $\{0.07, 0.10, 0.15, 0.25\}$ (Steps $\{4, 5, 6, 8\}$)
> **"Originally, the F5-TTS Teacher (Chen et al., 2025) used a 32-step grid (64 passes under Classifier-Free Guidance; Ho & Salimans, 2022) and showed that cutting it in half to a 16-step sway grid gives the same speech quality, while DMOSpeech 1 (Li et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2024) compressed the Teacher into a fast 4-step Student trained on four discrete noise levels starting at Step 4 ($t_4 \approx 0.08$). In DMOSpeech 2 (Li et al., 2026), the paper's Section 4.1 described the handover on the 32-step grid at `0.25` (14 Teacher steps + 2 Student steps), but their released `infer.py` code uses the 16-step Teacher grid with 3 Student steps—where `0.25` stops at Step 8 ($t_8 \approx 0.29$, half of 16) and costs 21 passes: 16 passes for the 8 Teacher Euler steps, plus 2 extra Teacher passes at Step 8 to compute the final endpoint estimate handed to the Student (`16 + 2 = 18` Teacher passes; Lipman et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026), plus 3 Student passes (`18 + 3 = 21` passes total). Because DMOSpeech 2 only tested `0.25` (Step 8) in Table 1—even though their own code defaults to `0.07` (Step 4, 13 passes) and their Section 3.3.2 suggests `0.10–0.20` (Steps 5 and 6 at `0.10` and `0.15`, 15 and 17 passes; matching the 20%–40% early switching window of Pan et al., 2025) for efficiency—our thesis tests all four candidates $\{0.07, 0.10, 0.15, 0.25\}$: we exclude Steps 1–3 because the Teacher has not reached the Student's trained starting level $\tau_1 \approx 0.08$ at Step 4 yet (Yin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025), we skip Step 7 (19 passes) because it saves only 2 passes over the zero-failure Step 8 reference (21 passes), and we do not go past Step 8 because DMOSpeech 2 Table 1 (Li et al., 2026), Shallow Flow Matching (Yang et al., 2025), and F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025) show Step 8 ($t \approx 0.25\text{--}0.29$) already beats the full Teacher and later Teacher work is washed out when the Student re-noises the handover state back to $\tau_1 \approx 0.08$ (Meng et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2024)."**

### 1B. Why `0.25` (Step 8) is a Valid Standard Quality Cap—and Why Its Problem is Purely Computational Cost
> **"Across related literature operating on the same $t \in [0, 1]$ flow-matching TTS trajectory, $t \approx 0.25\text{--}0.29$ (Step 8 of 16, $t_8 = 0.2929$) is an established, high-quality standard ceiling because the $0 \to 1$ ODE trajectory is curved only during the early phase ($t \lesssim 0.25\text{--}0.29$) and becomes nearly linear afterward (Zheng et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025), which is why DMOSpeech 2's $t_{\text{switch}} = 0.25$ (Step 8) already outperforms the 32-step F5-TTS Teacher on WER (`1.738%` vs. `1.947%`) and SIM (`0.699` vs. `0.662`) while recovering 89.1% of Teacher pitch diversity (Li et al., 2026). Therefore, `0.25` (Step 8) is NOT a speech-quality problem, but a COMPUTATIONAL COST problem: because `0.25` (Step 8) is a fixed worst-case upper bound needed when zero-shot prompts are noisy or complex (Wang et al., 2024; Starodubcev et al., 2024), DMOSpeech 2 forces every sentence—even clean, simple sentences—to run all 8 guided Teacher steps (**21 neural passes**; Ho & Salimans, 2022; Li et al., 2026), when easy/clean utterances already finish their curved phase and converge by **Steps 4–6** ($t \approx 0.07\text{--}0.15$, **13–17 passes**), where $0 \to 1$ flow-matching TTS models already match or exceed the `0.25` quality ceiling (Yang et al., 2025; Castillo et al., 2025; Schuster et al., 2022)."**
