EXP-022 (EVAL-015, run 2026-11-13 UTC on a Colab Tesla T4, 360 of 360 cases and 40 of 40 diversity pairs, results with SHA-256 hashes in [EXP-022](../../research/experiments/EXP-022-slow-part-handover-prelim/README.md); development evidence, white-noise prompts) tests Slow-Part Handover. The teacher stops at the first step from Step 2 where the slow part of its estimate has settled, and hands over only that part; a one-pass student draft supplies the rest. **It does not advance under its preregistration (`c311aeb52d424344...`): C2 and C4 pass, C1 and C3 fail.**
- **Why:** on noisy15 prompts the reference's outputs had no trackable pitch for any of the 20 speakers (UTMOS 1.25), so pitch variety at Step 3 could not be computed. Steps 2 and 3 were dropped, and the cheapest stop (Step 4) cost the same 14 passes as Voice-Swap.
- **A design weakness:** EXP-020 measured pitch variety on clean prompts only, and noisy15 was added without checking that the reference could be tracked there.
- **How the rule behaved:** C4 passed by 2 passes in 360 cases. The quality limit never bound, so every noisy input stopped at Step 4.
- **Diagnostics after the results:** with Step 3 allowed, the rule would cost 13.08 passes within the quality margins, but with higher WER than Voice-Swap (+0.0084 [+0.0013, +0.0159]). Random routing with the same stops per condition does as well, so the signal does not pick the right inputs.
- **Not done:** no retuning on these data.
