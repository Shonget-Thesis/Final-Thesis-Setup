## R03: where DMOSpeech 2 discusses 0.25, and whether it was shown to be best

Checked in arXiv:2507.14988v1 (20 Jul 2025, 21 pages), from the PDF text:

- **Section 4.1, "Baselines", p. 9** is the only place 0.25 appears. Configuration (5) reads "(tswitch = 0.25, with teacher handling 14 steps and student handling 2 steps, for a total of 16 steps)".
- **Tables 1 and 2 (pp. 9–10), Section 4.3.3 and Figure 3 (p. 11)** each report this single teacher-guided setting ("16 steps").
- **Section 3.3.2, p. 8** suggests "around 0.4-0.5" when diversity is critical and "around 0.1-0.2" when efficiency is paramount, without results for either range. 0.25 lies in neither range.
- **Algorithm 2, p. 8** runs the teacher to the last grid point with t_k ≤ t_switch.
- **Answer:** 0.25 was a chosen setting, not one shown to be the best. The paper gives no reason for it and evaluates no other value.

**Released code.** Checked in the official repository github.com/yl4579/DMOSpeech2 (HEAD 10c2255, 19 commits), not the vendored copy:

- `src/infer.py` `generate()` defaults to `teacher_steps=16, teacher_stopping_time=0.07, student_start_step=1`, unchanged since the first commit. That is 8 steps and 13 forward passes.
- `src/demo.ipynb` calls this "Basic Inference (teacher-guided, 8 steps)". It runs the paper's 16-step setting with `teacher_steps=24, teacher_stopping_time=0.3, student_start_step=2`.
- The vendored `code/vendor/dmospeech2` contains an `app_gradio.py` that the official repository does not.

**Not yet checked:** the AAAI 2026 version (vol. 40, pp. 31814–31822, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v40i38.40450), which the thesis cites. The publisher's site blocks automated access, so its wording and pages must be confirmed in a browser.
